# BSLT stack fit review — live-first hybrid creator platform

> Rebuilt from the saved results of the 2026-11-10 stack-fit workflow (6 lens reviewers, 1 contrarian challenger, 24 vendor fact-checks, synthesis). Recommendations were later re-scored under the owner's "own all runtime code except React" rule — see `ownership-rescore.md`.

## Bottom line

Yes, the core stack is right for this product, and you should keep it: TypeScript, Node 24, Fastify 5, Postgres with hand-written SQL, the React 19/Vite SPA, @bslt/ui, the typed contracts and generated client, the Postgres job queue, and Turborepo with pnpm. None of the alternatives considered (Next.js, NestJS with an ORM, Phoenix, writing your own Go services, Supabase) makes the hard parts easier, and each would throw away about 250k lines of tested TypeScript. The hard parts are live video, high-risk payments, age checks, moderation, and vendors whose terms leave adult content to their discretion.

It is still a SaaS starter, though, and several things must change before launch:

- **Payments.** Stripe prohibits this content category, and PayPal is closed to it by default. There is no ledger, wallet, pay-per-view purchase, tip or payout support anywhere in the repo.
- **Realtime.** It only signals clients to refetch, with no message content. Fan-out goes through Postgres NOTIFY, and any logged-in user can subscribe to almost any channel.
- **Live video.** There is none.
- **Media.** Uploads are held in API memory, processing jobs are never saved, there is no CDN or signed delivery, and location data stays on uploaded originals.
- **Row-level security does nothing at runtime.** I checked the code: nothing switches to the restricted role, production connects as the postgres superuser, and only migration 0901 forces RLS.
- **Deploys.** Every deploy runs compose down, which takes the database down too, on a host shared with your other products.
- **Trust and safety.** The only age check is a 13+ checkbox. There is no content rating, no moderation case tracking, and no identity or consent records for creators.

It would be a mistake to change the framework, add an ORM, move money workflows off Postgres, write your own media server or second-language services, or keep Stripe or PayPal for fan payments.

## Top decisions

### Live video vendor for the MVP

- Options: AWS IVS Real-Time plus Low-Latency (managed) · Self-hosted LiveKit OSS with Ingress and Egress · LiveKit Cloud, Mux or Cloudflare Stream/Realtime · Agora or Dolby
- Recommendation: Use AWS IVS behind a LiveVideoProvider port, and move to self-hosted LiveKit in phase 2 to cut cost. Use LiveKit Cloud, Mux or Cloudflare for SFW only, unless they confirm softcore in writing. Don't use Agora or Dolby.
- Why: AWS is the only managed vendor whose written terms are clean (fact-check: yes, medium confidence). Self-hosting costs about 5 to 7 times less per viewer-hour, but needs an adult-tolerant host with cheap bandwidth, which isn't cleared yet, plus someone on call for the SFU during paid shows.

### Fan payment processing

- Options: CCBill as primary plus Segpay or Verotel as secondary · Segpay as primary · A separate SFW-only entity on Stripe Connect · Keep Stripe/PayPal for the SFW side in the same entity
- Recommendation: CCBill plus a second processor. Turn Stripe and PayPal off for fan payments in the fork. Consider a separate SFW entity only with counsel's advice.
- Why: Stripe prohibits adult services, including adult live chat and pay-per-view, and judges the business as a whole. PayPal is closed by default and holds funds for 180 days or more. Segpay's site-wide rules would also apply to SFW creators. Verotel only offers webcam billing after 6 months of processing history.

### Fee model versus processing cost

- Options: Fees on top: token-pack prices include processing · Deduct processing from creator gross · Platform absorbs processing
- Recommendation: Put fees on top. Price token packs and subscriptions to cover processing, and keep the 10 to 15% platform take on creator earnings.
- Why: Published high-risk costs run 13 to 15.5%, plus a 5 to 10% reserve held for about 6 months. The CCBill figures the reviewer cited couldn't be verified, so get written quotes.

### Crypto scope at launch

- Options: Crypto pay-in for token packs only through a cleared provider; USDC payouts once a provider confirms · USDC payouts at launch through an unconfirmed provider · Direct on-chain payouts from our own wallets
- Recommendation: Crypto pay-in for token packs only, through a cleared provider. Launch with fiat payouts, using the processor's payout platform, ACH or wire, and Paxum for non-US creators. Add USDC payouts once a custodial provider confirms in writing. Never self-custody at MVP.
- Why: Coinbase and BitPay prohibit the category. NOWPayments excludes EU, UK, US, UAE and Russian residents. Circle is unclear and can freeze USDC.

### How strongly to separate SFW and softcore

- Options: Same app, but a separate softcore hostname, buckets, CDN zones and processor site, with content mode enforced by RLS · A path such as /after/* on one origin, filtered on the server · Separate legal entities and brands
- Recommendation: Separate hostname, buckets, zones and processor site now. Get counsel's view on separate entities before launch.
- Why: A separate hostname isolates cookies, caches, the CSP, analytics and search classification. It also means one vendor's or processor's decision only affects one tier.

### Core hosting provider

- Options: DigitalOcean in a dedicated account, with written pre-clearance and no softcore media stored · AWS · GCP · Hetzner
- Recommendation: DigitalOcean if DO clears it in writing, otherwise AWS. Never use Hetzner for anything that touches softcore.
- Why: DigitalOcean's terms are restrictive and leave room for discretion. Hetzner bans pornographic and indecent content. With media on Bunny and video on IVS, the core host holds mostly metadata.

### Public-page rendering

- Options: Extend the existing SSR seam plus an edge Worker for Open Graph tags · Next.js · React Router v7 or TanStack Start · Client-only SPA
- Recommendation: Extend the existing SSR seam and add the edge Worker.
- Why: Discovery depends on link previews and pages search engines can index. The framework options would replace the router and query layer. This still reverses the documented no-SSR stance, which you need to accept.

### Minimum age across the platform

- Options: 18+ everywhere · 13+/16+ on SFW with no DMs or tipping for minors
- Recommendation: 18+ everywhere.
- Why: Paid DMs, tipping and private shows next to an adult-adjacent section greatly widen child-safety exposure. GCP's generative-AI terms also bar services likely to be used by under-18s.

### Make RLS real now

- Options: Enforce it: separate roles, FORCE, tests, staged rollout · Document isolation as application-only
- Recommendation: Enforce it.
- Why: It is the strongest backstop for keeping softcore away from SFW users and for isolating the ledger, and right now the superuser connection bypasses it.

### Redis in the creator fork

- Options: Required, managed with high availability · Optional, keep Postgres NOTIFY
- Recommendation: Required, managed with high availability.
- Why: Live-room fan-out, presence and rate limits need it. NOTIFY doesn't scale to chat and tip rates.

### server-rust

- Options: Extract it to its own repo with history · Leave it in place, excluded by profile
- Recommendation: Extract it, coordinated with the Kanu workflow.
- Why: It has nothing to do with this product and blurs the foundation's single-backend story.

### New dependencies

- Options: hls.js and the IVS SDK now · OTel SDK, PDQ hashing and tus/Uppy later
- Recommendation: Approve only hls.js and the chosen video SDK for the creator MVP, and defer the rest.
- Why: Each new npm package needs your approval, so the list is kept short on purpose.

(main/server/db/migrations/0000_users.sql (user_role enum); main/server/core/src/admin/userService.ts; main/server/core/src/notifications/admin-alerts.ts; grep found no report/abuse/moderation tables)

(main/server/media/src/security.ts, processor.ts, image-processing.ts (withMetadata option))

### Verdicts

- **Age-assurance port + records (generic)** — add-new, foundation-now, M. Add an AgeAssuranceProvider port in server/core (startCheck/handleWebhook/getResult) and an age_assurance_checks table: user_id, provider, method (facial_estimation|document|eid_wallet|credit_card|reusable_token), result_over_threshold bool, threshold, assurance_level, jurisdiction, provider_ref, checked_at, expires_at. Store no DOB and no document images. Add an 'age_verified_for(threshold)' entitlement helper in shared that every gated route uses server-side. Design it to accept EUDI-wallet / EU age-app attestations later. Make MINIMUM_SIGNUP_AGE configurable per fork instead of a constant.
  - Requirement: Age verification for the opt-in softcore mode: US state AV laws (upheld in Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton, 2025), UK OSA 'highly effective age assurance' (enforced since 25 Jul 2025, Part 3 user-to-user services in scope), EU DSA Art. 28 guidelines (Jul 2025)
  - Rationale: The only age signal today is a 13+ self-attestation. That is not highly effective age assurance under Ofcom guidance and not age verification under US state laws. The foundation should hold a provider-agnostic port and an evidence table so any fork can gate age-restricted features (adult, alcohol, gambling-adjacent, etc.). Store the outcome, not the identity documents.
  - Risks: Webhook spoofing (verify signatures). Re-verification cadence and token reuse rules differ by jurisdiction. New dependency/vendor needs owner approval.
  - Alternatives: Vendor-hosted-only flow with no local record (loses audit evidence); Credit-card-as-age-proof (weak; not HEAA in UK on its own for all cases)
- **Viewer age-assurance vendor (softcore gate)** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Integrate one vendor offering facial age estimation with a document fallback and a reusable age token, e.g. Yoti or Ondato, through the foundation port. Make the softcore opt-in a server-side user setting (content_mode = softcore) that requires a valid check plus an allowed jurisdiction. Fail closed: no check means SFW only. Set the facial-estimation buffer age conservatively; Ofcom reportedly found OnlyFans' threshold misconfigured.
  - Requirement: SFW users must never see softcore; age-gated opt-in softcore mode; UK/US-state/EU compliance
  - Rationale: Erotic content made 'principally for sexual arousal' meets the OSA s.236 definition of pornographic content even without explicit nudity. Ofcom carves out only glamour content that falls short of that purpose, so the UK softcore section needs HEAA. The Ofcom fines so far (e.g. AVS Group £1M) show active enforcement. US state laws trigger on a share of 'harmful to minors' material, so gating all softcore behind verified age is the simple and defensible policy. Vendors that already serve live and creator platforms in this category: Yoti (works with OnlyFans), Ondato (public OnlyFans/Fansly case studies), Incode (adult-entertainment vertical, Chaturbate case study).
  - Risks: Cost per check (Yoti per-check pricing is not public; one UK G-Cloud listing shows about £1.80 per ID verification). Conversion drop at the gate. Per-state US legal review still needed. VPN evasion: geo is a signal, not proof.
  - Alternatives: Incode; Persona (adult entertainment allowed as a 'regulated industry' with conditions); Veriff (adult stance unclear); VerifyMy / AgeChecked (adult-specialist AV providers)
- **Creator / performer identity, age and consent records** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Creator KYC through document + selfie liveness, using the same vendor as age assurance where possible. Add verified_persons (vendor ref, verified_at, 18+ confirmed, document country/type, no image copies; vendor holds artifacts), performer_releases (person, release version, e-sign evidence, signed_at, revoked_at), and a content_performers join on every post, media asset, recording and live session. Block publishing to softcore unless every tagged performer is verified and has a current release. Add a co-performer invite flow. Restrict content policy to forbid anything that would trigger 2257 (no exposed genitals or pubic area, no simulated sex acts) while keeping 2257-grade records anyway. Put release PDFs in a separate restricted bucket with KMS and access logging.
  - Requirement: Creator identity/age verification and consent records for anyone appearing in softcore content (card-network rules; 2257-style hedging); AI/nonhuman performers still need an accountable verified human
  - Rationale: 18 U.S.C. 2257/2257A cover actual and simulated sexually explicit conduct (and lascivious exhibition of genitals or pubic area). Lingerie or implied nudity with no simulated sex and no exhibition of genitals or pubic area likely falls outside, but the reasonable-viewer test makes borderline erotic content risky, so counsel must confirm. Separately, Mastercard's adult-content standards (effective Oct 2021) require uploads only from verified providers, age and identity verification of everyone depicted, consent documentation, pre-publication review, and a complaint/appeal path. High-risk acquirers enforce these. Category practice is a government ID plus a signed release for every co-performer, often through an invite/e-sign link (ManyVids, OnlyFans, Pornhub expiring manual forms after 2 years). Nothing like this exists in the repo.
  - Risks: Highest-sensitivity PII in the system; a breach is existential. Release revocation must trigger takedown. Retention must outlive account deletion (see legal-hold verdict). Legal counsel needed on 2257 scope and on consent wording.
  - Alternatives: Ondato / Incode / Persona / Veriff / Yoti IDV; Manual document review (does not scale; higher breach exposure)
- **Retention defaults / legal hold** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Add a generic legal_holds table (subject type/id, reason, held_until) and a per-category retention policy registry. Make anonymization and purge jobs skip held subjects and trust-and-safety/compliance categories. Enable archive-before-delete for the audit categories a fork marks as evidentiary.
  - Requirement: Consent and verification evidence, CSAM report preservation, TAKE IT DOWN request trails, moderation decisions
  - Rationale: Current defaults would destroy evidence the creator platform must keep. Audit rows purge at 90 days, hard-banned users are anonymized after 7 days, and PII grace is 30 days. The REPORT Act (2024) requires CyberTipline report materials to be preserved for 1 year, secured to NIST CSF. Performer and consent records must outlive account deletion. The retention machinery itself is sound and reusable.
  - Risks: Must reconcile with GDPR erasure. Hold scope needs a documented lawful basis (legal obligation / legal claims).
  - Alternatives: Separate WORM archive bucket (S3 Object Lock) for evidentiary exports
- **Audit log** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Extend audit_category with trust_safety and compliance. Log every view or download of KYC/release records. Revoke UPDATE/DELETE on audit_events from the app DB role.
  - Requirement: Moderation, KYC-access and takedown audit trails
  - Rationale: The append-only, typed audit log is the right base. The category enum lacks trust-and-safety and compliance categories, and nothing records who viewed sensitive verification records.
  - Risks: Enum migrations need care with existing CHECK on action format (fine).
- **Automated content classification (images/video/text)** — add-new, foundation-now, M. Foundation: a ModerationProvider port (scan(assetRef) returns normalized labels and scores), a provider-to-platform taxonomy mapper (sfw | suggestive | softcore | explicit_prohibited | csam_suspect | violence | other), and a pipeline on the existing Postgres job queue. Creator MVP: Hive as primary (image + video + text + CSAM through Thorn in one contract) or Sightengine for finer lingerie/suggestive granularity, with thresholds tuned on a labeled sample. Any 'explicit' or 'csam_suspect' result blocks publication and opens a human case.
  - Requirement: Automated moderation; content-rating enforcement (SFW vs suggestive/softcore vs prohibited explicit)
  - Rationale: The media scanner checks only file type and size. The platform needs a fine distinction: lingerie and implied nudity are allowed in softcore, explicit content is banned everywhere, and SFW must stay clean. Sightengine nudity-2.1 has 19 suggestive classes (lingerie, bikini, cleavage tiers). AWS Rekognition's v7 taxonomy has Non-Explicit Nudity, Implied Nudity and Swimwear or Underwear at roughly $0.001/image. Hive offers NSFW/suggestive heads and bundles Thorn CSAM hash + classifier. Google SafeSearch returns only adult/racy likelihoods, which is too coarse for this split.
  - Risks: Classifier false negatives on edge content, so pre-publication human review is still needed for softcore. Per-call cost on video. Vendor sees all content (DPA required).
  - Alternatives: Sightengine; AWS Rekognition (cheapest; coarser suggestive classes); Google Cloud Vision SafeSearch (secondary signal only)
- **CSAM detection and NCMEC reporting** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Thorn Safer (directly or through the Hive integration) for hash match plus classifier on every upload, recording and live thumbnail. Register as an ESP with the NCMEC CyberTipline. Build a reporting workflow (quarantine, preserve, report, legal hold) with restricted reviewer access.
  - Requirement: Automated + human moderation; legal reporting duty for US electronic service providers (18 U.S.C. 2258A as amended by the REPORT Act 2024)
  - Rationale: This is non-negotiable for any UGC platform with suggestive content and live video. The REPORT Act changed the trigger to awareness of an apparent violation, raised fines to $600k–$1M, and requires 1-year preservation of reported material secured to NIST CSF. Hash matching against known CSAM plus a novel-content classifier is standard practice (Thorn Safer, PhotoDNA).

### Gaps

- No real age assurance: only a 13+ self-attestation with no DOB and no verification. Nothing meets UK OSA 'highly effective age assurance', US state AV laws or the EU DSA Art. 28 expectations for the softcore section.
- No creator KYC, no performer/co-performer identity and age records, and no model-release/consent records linking every depicted person to each piece of content (needed under the Mastercard adult-content standards and as a 2257 hedge).
- No content rating field anywhere, and no structural (query/RLS/CDN) guarantee that SFW viewers never receive softcore. Feature flags cannot target per user or per jurisdiction.
- No jurisdiction/geo resolution to apply per-state and per-country age-assurance and availability rules.
- No content classifier: the media scanner checks only size, MIME type and magic bytes, with no nudity/suggestive/explicit detection on images, video or live streams.
- No CSAM hash matching or novel-CSAM classifier, and no NCMEC CyberTipline reporting/preservation workflow (REPORT Act: 1-year preservation, NIST CSF security).
- No user content reports, moderation cases/queues, SLAs, appeals or statements of reasons. Moderation exists only as account lock/ban.
- No TAKE IT DOWN Act notice-and-removal flow (48h SLA, removal of identical copies), no DMCA flow, and no perceptual hashing to block re-uploads of removed media.
- No AI/synthetic performer model, labeling or likeness-consent records (EU AI Act Art. 50(4) applies since 2 Aug 2026).
- Retention defaults (audit 90d purge, hard-ban anonymization after 7d, PII grace 30d) would destroy trust-and-safety and compliance evidence; there is no legal-hold mechanism.
- Audit categories lack trust_safety/compliance, and access to sensitive verification records is not logged.
- Search is admin-only ILIKE over the users table. There is no public content/creator search, FullText is unsupported, and there are no tsvector/pg_trgm indexes.
- No discovery ranking, follows, categories, recommendation signals, mid-tier exposure mechanics or referral attribution.
- Analytics: the events partition-maintenance job was never wired (all rows go to events_default after month two). Logged-out/anonymous viewers can't be tracked, there are no server-side canonical revenue events, no rollups, no creator-facing analytics, and only a log exporter.
- No handling of GDPR Art. 9 special-category data (softcore opt-in and viewing history likely count as data concerning sex life): no segregation, explicit-consent type or minimum-count aggregation for creator audience reports.
- The AUP/ToS templates have no adult/suggestive content policy, explicit-content ban list, creator agreement or performer release document types.
- The ffmpeg transcode path does not strip video container metadata (GPS/device), a creator-safety privacy gap.
- No DSA/OSA transparency-reporting data model (counts of notices, actions and appeals by category).

## Media pipeline, storage/CDN, and content protection: uploads, transcoding/VOD, object storage + CDN egress, signed delivery, blurred/teaser previews, watermarking/DRM, CSAM hash-matching, and EXIF/metadata stripping.

### Observed

- **Upload transport**: Uploads go through the API. Fastify's multipart parser buffers the whole request body in memory (addContentTypeParser with parseAs 'buffer'), then the server calls storage.upload(key, Buffer). The media route allows 200 MB (MEDIA_MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES) and the media constants allow 100 MB. There are no direct-to-bucket, presigned-PUT, S3 multipart, or tus uploads, and uploads cannot resume. (main/apps/server/src/plugin/multipart.ts; main/apps/server/src/http/media/types.ts (MEDIA_MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES = 200 MiB); main/apps/server/src/http/media/routes.ts; main/server/core/src/files/service.ts uploadFile(); grep found no CreateMultipartUpload/tus anywhere)
- **@bslt/storage**: There are two providers: an S3-compatible one (@aws-sdk/client-s3; PutObject from a Buffer; presigned GET URLs that last 3600 s by default; getSignedUploadUrl exists in the wrapper but nothing exposes it through StorageProvider) and a local-disk one, which uses the HMAC-signed /uploads/:id/:filename file server with Cache-Control private max-age of 60 days. There is no CDN layer, no multipart support, no copy/move between prefixes, and no checksums. allowedTypes defaults to ['*']. The files table CHECK constraint allows 'gcs', but no GCS provider is implemented. (main/server/storage/src/providers/s3.ts, s3.client.wrapper.ts, local.ts, config.ts; main/apps/server/src/http/file-server.ts:262-360; docs/DIRECTION.md:185)
- **Media Studio (what is actually wired)**: apps/server/src/http/media reuses @bslt/core/files for upload, list, get, and delete. It lazy-loads only four sharp-based functions from @bslt/media (getImageFormat, validateImage, resizeImage, optimizeImage) for image probe and transform. Access is owner-or-admin only. The web client plays the original uploaded file as progressive download (<video src={downloadUrl}>). There is no HLS player. (main/apps/server/src/http/media/service.ts, engine.ts; main/apps/web/src/features/media/pages/MediaStudioPage.tsx:105)

It is not production-ready: createHLSStream passes no HLS options (no -hls_time, no rendition ladder, no encryption, and the segmentsDir it creates is never used). generateThumbnail ignores its time argument. VideoProcessor.process returns a thumbnailPath without generating a thumbnail. ffmpeg copies global metadata by default and no -map_metadata -1 is passed. checkFFmpeg always returns true. Processing only works on local file paths. Nothing in the app calls any of it. (main/server/media/src/ffmpeg-wrapper.ts (createHLSStream, generateThumbnail, checkFFmpeg); main/server/media/src/processors/video.ts; grep shows no app imports of VideoProcessor, createHLS or ServerMediaQueue)

ServerMediaQueue.processJobData only logs the result: it never uploads processed outputs back to storage and never writes processingStatus to the database. The @bslt/media/http uploadMedia hardcodes storageProvider 'local' and passes an S3 key as a local filePath. This http module duplicates the app's own media routes and is not mounted. Separately, a durable Postgres job_queue already exists (0405_job_queue.sql, used by PostgresQueueStore) and the media code does not use it. It has no status or state-machine column, no checksum or perceptual hash, no variants or renditions, no visibility or entitlement fields, no content rating, no moderation fields, and no legal-hold or retention fields. ### Verdicts

- **Upload ingestion (API-buffered multipart)** — replace, foundation-now, M. Foundation: an upload-session API in server-core. Images and small files use a presigned PUT whose content type and length are pinned in the signature. Large files use S3 multipart with per-part presigned URLs: the client re-requests only missing parts to resume, and the server verifies with HEAD plus a checksum on completion. Objects land in a private 'incoming/' quarantine prefix, and completion enqueues an ingest job. Add a small chunked uploader to client/api. Creator fork: once a managed video vendor is chosen, video uses that vendor's direct-creator tus upload instead.
  - Requirement: Creators upload photos, video posts and multi-GB stream recordings from mobile and desktop web; uploads must survive flaky mobile connections.
  - Rationale: The current parser holds the whole body in Node memory (up to 200 MB per request) and cannot resume, so a few concurrent video uploads can exhaust API memory. Uploading straight to the bucket takes media bytes off the API entirely. The existing @aws-sdk/client-s3 already supports CreateMultipartUpload, UploadPart, CompleteMultipartUpload and AbortMultipartUpload, so the generic path needs no new server package.
  - Risks: Buckets need CORS. Abandoned multipart uploads need a lifecycle abort rule. R2 presigned URLs work only on the S3 API domain, not custom domains, and R2 does not support HTML-form POST uploads. Quotas must be enforced when the session is created, because the bytes never pass through the API.
  - Alternatives: tus server (@tus/server + @tus/s3-store), new npm packages that need owner approval; Uppy client with @uppy/aws-s3 multipart (new client dependency); Vendor tus endpoints: Cloudflare Stream direct creator uploads (tus required above 200 MB) or Bunny Stream presigned tus
- **@bslt/storage provider abstraction** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Add stream bodies, presigned PUT and multipart, head, copy/move between prefixes or buckets, and a SHA-256 checksum. Make objects private by default. Route each object to a named bucket class (originals, quarantine, published-public, published-locked) instead of one bucket. Remove 'gcs' or implement it. Use the local HMAC file server only in development. For locked content, replace its 60-day private max-age with short TTLs.
  - Requirement: Scale for video storage, a quarantine-then-publish flow, multiple access classes (public, subscriber, PPV, softcore), and a generic foundation that can be reused.
  - Rationale: An S3-compatible port is the right abstraction, because R2, B2, Bunny, Wasabi, DO Spaces and S3 all speak it. What it lacks is streaming, multipart, copy/move between prefixes and checksums, and some leftovers are inconsistent: the 'gcs' value in the CHECK constraint has no implementation, @bslt/media/http hardcodes 'local', and allowedTypes defaults to '*'.
  - Risks: Moving objects between buckets costs Class A operations on R2. Cross-vendor copies are not atomic, so the database must remain the source of truth for an object's state.
  - Alternatives: Keep a single bucket and separate access classes by prefix only (simpler, but makes per-class vendor moves and per-class lifecycle rules harder)
- **Delivery URL signing (CDN access control)** — add-new, foundation-now, M. A DeliverySigner port in the foundation: getDeliveryUrl(keyOrPrefix, {viewerId, ttlSec, rating}) returning tokens that live for minutes. The default adapter is S3 presign (development and small apps). Creator-fork adapters: Bunny CDN token authentication (works for HLS playlists and segments), Cloudflare custom-domain HMAC via the WAF function is_timed_hmac_valid_v0 (Pro plan or higher) or a Worker, or CloudFront signed cookies. Server-core checks entitlement (subscription, PPV purchase, age-verified softcore mode) before signing. Raw bucket URLs are never returned for locked content.
  - Requirement: Locked and PPV content must be hard to leak; SFW users must never receive softcore URLs; video segments must be cacheable at the CDN.
  - Rationale: Today every access is an S3 presigned GET valid for one hour, served directly from the bucket with no CDN, and the link can be shared for that whole hour. On R2, presigned URLs cannot use custom domains, so they bypass the Cloudflare cache. HLS needs a token that covers a path prefix, because there are hundreds of segment URLs per video. Signing must therefore be a separate port from storage.
  - Risks: HMAC tokens on Cloudflare need a paid zone plan. Signing a whole directory lets any holder fetch any file under the prefix during the TTL, so keep TTLs short and prefixes narrow.
  - Alternatives: Per-viewer HLS manifest rewriting by the API, signing every segment URL (works with any S3 origin, but every playlist request hits the API); Vendor-native signed playback (Mux signed playback IDs, Cloudflare Stream signed tokens, Bunny embed tokens) for video only
- **Object storage and CDN vendor (currently DO Spaces / S3-generic, no CDN)** — replace, creator-mvp, M. Creator MVP: Bunny.net (Storage, CDN with token auth, and Stream) as the default media vendor. Use separate storage zones and pull zones for SFW and for softcore, so the softcore tier can move vendors without touching SFW. Fallback: Cloudflare R2 + CDN + Stream for SFW, or for everything if Cloudflare confirms the softcore use case in writing. Backblaze B2 behind a Bunny CDN is a viable low-cost origin for originals and archives.
  - Requirement: Scale for video storage and egress; a vendor whose acceptable-use policy tolerates suggestive and softcore content; web delivery on mobile and desktop.
  - Rationale: At video volume, egress dominates the bill: S3 with CloudFront or DO Spaces charges per GB delivered, while R2 charges no egress ($0.015/GB-month standard storage) and Bunny bills cheap per-GB storage plus CDN. Cloudflare's rules allow video and large files through its CDN only when the content is hosted on Stream, R2 or Images, so putting a CDN in front of DO Spaces through the existing Cloudflare setup is not an option. On acceptable use, Bunny's AUP explicitly accepts legal adult content. Cloudflare's terms are content-neutral but reserve discretion over 'harmful' content. DO and GCP do not ban consensual adult content outright, but the policies are non-exhaustive. Wasabi's terms could not be verified, and it is not built to be a hot delivery origin.
  - Risks: Concentration and deplatforming risk in the adult-adjacent category; keep the storage port vendor-neutral and keep softcore isolated. Bunny is a smaller vendor, so check SLAs and regional performance for the target markets.
  - Alternatives: Cloudflare R2 + Cloudflare CDN (zero egress; get written confirmation for softcore first); Backblaze B2 as origin + Bunny CDN; AWS S3 + CloudFront signed cookies (most mature, highest egress cost); Wasabi (not recommended for delivery; AUP unverified)
- **VOD transcoding and HLS packaging (in-process ffmpeg)** — replace, creator-mvp, L. Managed VOD in the creator fork. Bunny Stream: free standard encoding, per-GB storage and CDN, tus uploads, CDN token auth, MediaCage Basic at no charge. Alternatively Cloudflare Stream: $5 per 1,000 minutes stored and $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered, free ingress and encoding, signed tokens, tus, watermark profiles, live recordings saved to VOD automatically. Choose it together with the live-streaming vendor so private and group show recordings land in the same library and can become PPV without re-uploading. The foundation keeps only a VideoHost port (createUpload, getPlayback, delete, webhook-to-status).
  - Requirement: Video posts and stream recordings sold as PPV, played on mobile and desktop web at adaptive bitrates.
  - Rationale: The wrapper is not a VOD pipeline. It produces no ABR ladder, sets no HLS parameters and no encryption, ignores the thumbnail time, does not strip metadata, writes outputs next to a local path without uploading them, and never runs because nothing calls it and production images ship without ffmpeg. Software x264 ABR transcoding on the API host would starve request handling. Managed VOD providers cover the encoding ladder, packaging, signed playback and players, and include live-to-VOD recording. Buying is clearly better than building at MVP scale.
  - Risks: Vendor lock-in of renditions (keep originals in your own bucket). Per-minute pricing grows with catalog size. Vendor AUP discretion over softcore content.
  - Alternatives: Mux Video (DRM generally available since Nov 2025, signed playback, strong developer experience; its terms let Mux refuse 'obscene or otherwise objectionable' content at its discretion, so get written approval first); AWS MediaConvert + S3/CloudFront (flexible, more glue code, egress cost); Self-hosted ffmpeg workers (GPU/NVENC) only later, if vendor per-minute costs dominate
- **FFmpeg wrapper for auxiliary jobs** — keep-with-changes, creator-mvp, M. Fix the bugs. Pass -ss for thumbnails. Add -map_metadata -1 -map_chapters -1 for privacy. Make checkFFmpeg actually detect a spawn failure. Remove createHLSStream, or implement it properly with hls_time, a playlist type and key info. Run it only in a separate media-worker container (ffmpeg plus a sharp/libvips image) that consumes the Postgres job queue, never in the API process.
  - Requirement: Blurred and teaser video previews, poster frames, probing, and metadata stripping that the vendor does not cover.
  - Rationale: A small, dependency-free wrapper with timeouts and buffer caps fits the owner's minimal-dependency principle well for bounded jobs. It needs bug fixes and a separate runtime that actually includes ffmpeg.
  - Risks: ffmpeg CVEs come with parsing untrusted media. Sandbox the worker: non-root, no network except storage, CPU and memory limits.
  - Alternatives: Vendor clipping APIs for teasers (Mux and Cloudflare Stream support clips), leaving ffmpeg only for blurred loops and probes
- **Media job pipeline (in-memory CustomJobQueue, orchestrator, facade, @bslt/media/http)** — replace, foundation-now, M. Foundation: run media jobs as named jobs on the existing PostgresQueueStore in a worker process. Drive them from an explicit state machine on the file record: uploaded → scanning → processing → pending_review → published | rejected | quarantined. Vendor webhooks advance the state. Delete the in-memory queue, the orchestrator and the duplicate HTTP module.
  - Requirement: Reliable ingest, scan, transcode and publish for every upload, with retries and visible status.
  - Rationale: Jobs live only in memory and results are never persisted. A durable Postgres job_queue with idempotency keys (0405, 0915) already exists and fits the owner's 'Postgres-first, minimal dependencies' direction. The @bslt/media/http module duplicates the app's real media routes and is dead code.
  - Risks: Postgres-queue throughput limits at very high upload rates; acceptable for the MVP, so watch queue latency metrics.
  - Alternatives: BullMQ on the optional Redis (new dependency; Redis is optional in this stack)
- **files schema (0600_files.sql)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Foundation (generic): status enum, sha256, a variants table (kind such as original, display, thumb, preview, blurred or hls_manifest, plus bucket class, key, dimensions and duration), access_class, a vendor asset id, and a legal_hold or retain_until timestamp. Creator fork: content_rating (sfw/softcore), links to posts, PPV and recordings, perceptual hash and scan results, and moderation decision references. Apply RLS to variant rows the same way as to files.
  - Requirement: Locked and PPV entitlements, SFW and softcore separation, previews and renditions, moderation and legal preservation.
  - Rationale: A single flat row with free-form JSONB metadata cannot express renditions, review state or access class, and the purpose CHECK constraint is too narrow for creator content.
  - Risks: A migration is needed for existing 'files' rows. Keep generic columns out of creator semantics to preserve reuse.
  - Alternatives: Separate media_assets table in the creator fork, leaving the generic files table untouched
- **Image pipeline (sharp)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. An ingest normalization job: magic-byte sniff using the existing detectFileType, then a decode-size limit, autoOrient/rotate, re-encode to WebP/AVIF/JPEG with metadata dropped, generate display widths and thumbnails, and write to the published bucket class. Keep the raw original only in a restricted 'originals' class that fans can never reach, or delete it after normalization. Add HEIC handling or require browser conversion, because prebuilt sharp may not decode HEIC.
  - Requirement: Photo posts, display variants for the existing <Image> srcset/cdnUrl hook, and creator privacy.
  - Rationale: sharp is the right tool, and by default it already drops metadata from derivatives. The gaps are that originals are kept raw (with GPS EXIF) and served, that orientation is lost because rotate/autoOrient is never called before metadata is stripped, and that file types are trusted from the client's declared MIME type.
  - Risks: Decompression bombs: enforce a pixel limit. Re-encoding costs CPU, so run it in the worker.
  - Alternatives: Cloudflare Images or Bunny Optimizer for on-the-fly variants (still normalize originals yourself first)
- **EXIF and metadata stripping (images and video)** — add-new, foundation-now, S. Make stripping a mandatory step in ingest normalization. For images, use sharp's default metadata drop after autoOrient. For video, run ffmpeg with -map_metadata -1 -map_chapters -1 (stream copy where possible) before sending to the vendor or storing, then verify with ffprobe that no location tags such as com.apple.quicktime.location remain. Never serve unnormalized originals.
  - Requirement: Creator privacy: no GPS, device or serial metadata leaks, which matters especially for softcore creators.
  - Rationale: Leaked location data is a real safety risk for creators in this category. Today originals carry full EXIF, and video gets no stripping at all. Vendor renditions are re-encoded, but any downloadable or original files must be stripped too.
  - Risks: Stream-copy remux may keep some container atoms; test with real iPhone and Android files.
  - Alternatives: Rely on vendor transcode only (insufficient if originals or MP4 downloads are ever served)
- **Blurred and teaser previews** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. For images, generate server-side with sharp: downscale heavily, blur, re-encode, and store as a separate object (never the same key or URL as the full asset). For video, use a short vendor clip or an ffmpeg teaser (first N seconds, or a blurred low-resolution loop) plus a poster frame. Previews of locked SFW content can be public. Softcore previews are served only from the softcore zone, signed for age-verified users in softcore mode, and never appear in SFW feeds, search, OG tags or sitemaps.
  - Requirement: Blurred or teaser previews for locked and softcore content; SFW users must never see softcore content.
  - Rationale: A CSS blur over the real asset ships the full image to the browser and leaks it. Previews must be separate, low-information objects with their own access class.
  - Risks: A preview at too high a resolution or too low a blur defeats the paywall. Preview generation must also wait for the CSAM and moderation result.
  - Alternatives: Vendor-generated animated thumbnails or storyboards (check that they cannot be requested at full resolution)
- **CSAM hash-matching, pre-publication review gate, and reporting** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Foundation: a ContentScanner port (scan(fileRef) → verdict) whose default is fail-closed, plus a publish gate in the state machine so nothing reaches 'published' without a passing scan and, where configured, an approved review. Creator fork: Thorn Safer for image and video scene hash matching (directly, or through Hive's combined CSAM API) on every image, video, recording and message attachment before publication. Add the PhotoDNA Cloud Service as a complement for images (free, but vetted access). Build an NCMEC CyberTipline reporting workflow, a quarantine bucket with restricted access and a legal hold, and an account-action hook. Cloudflare's CSAM Scanning Tool can be a free post-cache backstop only, because it scans images already served through the cache and does not work before publication.
  - Requirement: Legal must-have for nudity-adjacent UGC: hash-match every upload before publication. Card-network adult-merchant rules (Mastercard AN 5196 and Visa, per secondary sources) also require content review before publication, uploader and participant age/consent verification, and livestream monitoring.
  - Rationale: Nothing exists today. Hash matching catches only known material, so it must sit inside a blocking pending_review gate with human review. Reporting and evidence preservation are legal obligations on detection (Cloudflare's CSAM docs cite reporting, removal and one-year preservation). AI and nonhuman performers need classifier-based checks too, because synthetic material will not match known hashes.
  - Risks: Vendors may screen adult-adjacent platforms during onboarding, so start access applications early. Legal counsel should confirm the reporting and retention duties for each launch jurisdiction. Classifier and reviewer tooling belongs to the moderation lens but must share this gate.
  - Alternatives: Hive CSAM API (resells Thorn Safer matching plus classifiers); Microsoft PhotoDNA alone (images only, eligibility at Microsoft's discretion); Cloudflare CSAM Scanning Tool alone (insufficient: post-cache, images only)
- **Playback protection (HLS encryption, signed playback, DRM)** — augment, creator-mvp, M. MVP: vendor HLS with signed playback tokens (minutes-long TTL, bound to the viewer) and per-session encryption (Bunny MediaCage Basic at no charge, or the equivalent), with MP4 downloads disabled for locked assets. Phase 2: Widevine/FairPlay DRM for premium PPV and private-show recordings (Bunny MediaCage Enterprise at $99/month plus $0.005 per licence for the first 20k; or Mux DRM). An HLS player (hls.js or a vendor player) is a new client dependency that needs owner approval.
  - Requirement: Locked, PPV and private-show recordings must be hard to download or leak.
  - Rationale: Nothing protects playback today: the original MP4 is a progressive download through a one-hour signed URL. Short-lived tokens plus per-session encrypted HLS stop casual downloaders cheaply. Full multi-DRM costs money and still cannot stop screen recording (Bunny notes Widevine L3 devices cannot block capture beyond limiting to 480p), so it belongs to phase 2 for high-value items.
  - Risks: Clear-key encryption only deters casual downloading: an authorized viewer can extract the key. DRM adds licence cost and player complexity.
  - Alternatives: Self-hosted AES-128 HLS (ffmpeg -hls_key_info_file plus an entitlement-gated key endpoint) if not using a vendor; Mux signed playback plus DRM
- **Watermarking (visible per-viewer and forensic)** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. MVP: a dynamic overlay in the web player showing the viewer's handle and a short session code at a moving position, with the session code logged against the viewer. For PPV photos, burn a per-buyer watermark into the image with sharp composite when it is unlocked, cache it per buyer, and serve it through the signer. Pair this with a DMCA/takedown and repeat-leaker workflow. Later: session-based forensic watermarking from a vendor once leak volume justifies the cost.
  - Requirement: Deter leaks of paid content and attribute leaks to the account that leaked them.
  - Rationale: No DRM stops a phone pointed at a screen. A visible per-viewer mark deters leaking, and a stored mapping from session code to viewer makes leaks traceable for bans and takedowns. Forensic A/B segment watermarking (DoveRunner, Irdeto TraceMark) works but is enterprise-priced.
  - Risks: A player overlay can be removed by anyone who gets the raw stream, so it must be combined with signed or encrypted playback. Per-buyer image variants increase storage.
  - Alternatives: Cloudflare Stream watermark profiles (static per-video, not per-viewer); DoveRunner or Irdeto session-based A/B forensic watermarking (later)
- **Rating-segregated storage and delivery (SFW vs softcore)** — add-new, creator-mvp, S. A separate bucket (or storage zone) and a separate delivery hostname or pull zone for softcore assets. Delivery tokens carry a rating claim and are issued only to age-verified sessions in softcore mode. Content rating is set at upload and confirmed in review before publication. Reclassifying an asset re-keys or moves the object.
  - Requirement: Default SFW experience; the softcore mode is opt-in and age-gated; SFW users must never see softcore content.
  - Rationale: Enforcing the SFW/softcore split at the storage and CDN layer, rather than only in queries, means a bug in the feed or search layer cannot produce a working softcore URL for an SFW user. It also contains vendor and payment risk to one tier.
  - Risks: Reclassification flows and cache purges must be handled carefully, as must the case where a creator changes an asset's rating after publication.
  - Alternatives: Prefix-only separation in one bucket, with the signer enforcing rating (weaker but simpler)
- **Media infrastructure (Docker images, Terraform)** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. A media-worker image with ffmpeg and libvips, deployed as its own Compose service and Terraform resource. Terraform or vendor configuration for buckets per access class, CORS, a lifecycle rule to abort incomplete multipart uploads, retention for the quarantine bucket, and CDN token-auth zones. Prometheus metrics for queue depth and processing latency.
  - Requirement: Scalable media processing and storage that is reproducible.
  - Rationale: The production image has no ffmpeg, and Terraform provisions no buckets, CDN, lifecycle rules or worker pool. Media work must not share the API container.
  - Risks: Bunny and Cloudflare Terraform providers vary in maturity, so some configuration may stay manual.
  - Alternatives: Vendor-only processing with no worker until previews or watermarks are needed

### Gaps

- No resumable or direct-to-bucket uploads: everything is buffered through the API, with a 200 MB cap.
- No real video pipeline: no rendition ladder, HLS packaging, encryption or HLS player. The client plays the original file as progressive MP4.
- Media processing is not durable or persisted: an in-memory queue, results never written to storage or the database, and the ffmpeg path never called by the app.
- No CDN in front of object storage and no CDN token signing. Today's one-hour S3 presigned GET URLs are shareable, and on R2 they bypass the cache.
- Media access is owner-or-admin only: no subscriber, PPV or private-show entitlement check before a URL is issued.
- No blurred or teaser preview generation and no previews as separate objects.
- No CSAM hash matching, pre-publication review gate, NCMEC reporting workflow, or quarantine with legal hold.
- No visible or forensic watermarking and no leak-attribution log.
- Originals keep EXIF and GPS. No orientation handling, no video metadata stripping, and no magic-byte check on uploads.
- No perceptual hashing to block re-uploads of removed content or to find leaks.
- No SFW/softcore separation at the storage or delivery layer.
- No storage lifecycle rules, per-creator storage quotas, or egress metering tied to the existing metering tables.
- No Terraform or Compose definitions for buckets, CDN zones or a media worker, and ffmpeg is missing from the production image.
- Live-to-VOD (recording private and group shows into PPV) depends on the live-video vendor choice, which is outside this lens. Choose the VOD and live vendors together.

## Payments, ledger, and the data layer. Covers creator subscriptions, PPV, tips, paid messages, the 10–15% platform fee, payouts including USDT/USDC, chargebacks, refunds and reserves, high-risk processors, crypto pay-in, money and multi-currency handling, RLS for keeping SFW and softcore apart, and scaling. Postgres and custom SQL are a good fit for the ledger. The billing layer built around BillingService is the misfit: it is a single-provider, SaaS-subscription design with no ledger, wallet, payouts or one-time purchases. Its only adapters, Stripe and PayPal, both prohibit this content category. RLS is not enforced at runtime today.

### Observed

- **DB client / transactions**: postgres.js driver wrapped by RawDb (createRawDb/createDbFromSql). transaction() takes an isolation level (default read committed, serializable available), READ ONLY/DEFERRABLE, and uses savepoints when nested. withSession() wraps each query in BEGIN with SET LOCAL app.user_id, app.tenant_id and app.role (plus search_path in schema-per-tenant mode). QueryOptions.timeout is accepted but ignored (_options). There is no statement_timeout or lock_timeout and no helper that retries on serialization failure (40001/40P01). (main/server/db/src/client.ts:53-75 (IsolationLevel/TransactionOptions), 196-229 (applySession), 285-340 (transaction), query(..., _options) unused; grep shows no statement_timeout/lock_timeout anywhere in main/server or main/apps/server)
- **Query builder**: Custom select/insert/update/delete builder with forUpdate(), forShare(), ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING / DO UPDATE, RETURNING, union and window functions. SKIP LOCKED / NOWAIT are not in the builder; the queue uses raw SQL for them. (main/server/db/src/builder/select.ts:274-290, insert.ts:180-200; main/server/db/src/queue/postgres-store.ts:105-114 (FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED))
- **Billing schema**: Hand-written SQL tables: plans (a global catalog with TEXT ids, price_in_cents INTEGER, currency TEXT default 'usd', stripe__/paypal__ columns), subscriptions, customer_mappings, invoices (INTEGER amounts, CHECK >= 0), payment_methods, and billing_events (UNIQUE(provider, provider_event_id)). billing_provider is a Postgres ENUM limited to ('stripe','paypal'). payment_method_type includes 'crypto'. 0902 adds a nullable tenant_id and backfill audit but leaves RLS unchanged ('Phase 0'). (main/server/db/migrations/0200_billing.sql:11-19, 24-40, 147-157; 0902_billing_tenant_scope.sql header and ALTER TABLE statements)
- **Ledger / wallet / payouts**: None. No ledger, journal, posting, balance, wallet, payout, fee-split, reserve or connected-account tables or code exist. (grep -rliE 'ledger|payout|wallet|application_fee|double.entry' over main/**/*.ts|sql|rs matches only main/apps/storefront/src/readiness.ts (unrelated))
- **BillingService port**: A provider port shaped around SaaS subscriptions: createCustomer, createCheckoutSession(planId, priceId), createSubscription/cancel/resume/update, setup intents, attach/detach/default payment methods, listInvoices, product/price admin, verifyWebhookSignature(payload: Uint8Array, signature: string), and parseWebhookEvent returning one NormalizedWebhookEvent with 7 types (subscription.created/updated/canceled, invoice.paid/payment_failed, refund.created, chargeback.created). It has no one-time charge, dynamic-amount checkout, refund initiation, payout or fee split, no source-IP or query-string verification, and no synchronous response body. (main/shared/src/modules/core/billing/billing.service.types.ts:21-36 (CheckoutParams), 133-157 (event types), 203-259 (BillingService))
- **Provider factory**: Only one provider is active at a time: createBillingProvider switches on config.provider (stripe | paypal), with Mock and Disabled fallbacks. There is no routing, cascade or multi-processor support. (main/server/core/src/billing/factory.ts:128-151)
- **Webhook ingestion / idempotency**: processBillingWebhook runs inside one DB transaction: wasProcessed() check, then processEvent, then recordEvent. The UNIQUE(provider, provider_event_id) constraint stops concurrent duplicates by rolling the loser back. Notifications are collected and dispatched after commit. A chargeback or full refund revokes access by setting the subscription to canceled. Partial refunds are ignored. The webhook provider type is hard-coded to 'paypal' | 'stripe'. (main/server/core/src/billing/webhooks/shared.ts:31, 61-112; webhooks/stripe/events.ts:364-397)
- **Subscription cardinality**: The repository assumes one active subscription per user (findActiveByUserId ... LIMIT 1). That is the SaaS model. Fans with many creator subscriptions don't fit. (main/server/db/src/repositories/billing/subscriptions.ts:164-170)
- **RLS**: Every policy is declared TO authenticated (a NOLOGIN role created in 0900). The app never runs SET ROLE / SET LOCAL ROLE authenticated, and applySession sets only app.* GUCs. Production compose connects the API through PgBouncer as POSTGRES_USER, default 'postgres', which the postgres image creates as superuser. Superusers bypass RLS even under FORCE, and FORCE is set on only 4 tables. Net effect: RLS is decorative at runtime and isolation depends on application code. The real-DB tests exercise HTTP-level checks, not policies. (main/server/db/migrations/0900_rls.sql:11-14, 44-143; 0901_rls_tenant_trust_boundary.sql:150-153; main/server/db/src/client.ts:196-218; infra/docker/production/docker-compose.prod.yml:75, 111, 179-182; grep finds no 'SET ROLE' or GRANT authenticated anywhere)
- **Money handling**: Amounts are integers in cents (INTEGER, so the per-row maximum is about $21.4M). formatPrice divides by 100 and calls toFixed(2), with a '$' special case. (main/shared/src/modules/core/billing/billing.display.ts:33-39; 0200_billing.sql price_in_cents/amount_due INTEGER)
- **Job queue / scheduler**: A Postgres-backed queue in the same DB with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, exponential-backoff retries and an idempotency key (0915), plus a cron scheduler. It can serve as a transactional outbox for payouts and reconciliation. (main/server/db/src/queue/postgres-store.ts:6, 105-114; migrations/0405_job_queue.sql, 0915_job_queue_idempotency_key.sql, 0907_cron_jobs.sql)
- **Partitioning precedent**: The analytics events table uses native monthly RANGE partitioning on created_at, an ensure_events_partition() helper, a DEFAULT partition and PK (id, created_at). ### Verdicts

- **PostgreSQL as system of record (including the ledger)** — keep, foundation-now, S. Keep Postgres 16+ and put the ledger in it. Contention on hot accounts (platform revenue, processor clearing) is avoided by not updating a single balance row per tip: post to per-day or per-shard system sub-accounts, or compute system balances from postings, and lock only the fan wallet and the creator account.
  - Requirement: Double-entry ledger for subscriptions, PPV, tips, paid messages, platform fee, reserves and payouts; correctness over raw throughput
  - Rationale: The stack already has what a Postgres ledger needs: ACID transactions with selectable isolation, SELECT ... FOR UPDATE, UNIQUE constraints and ON CONFLICT for idempotency, savepoints, an in-DB job queue for a transactional outbox, and a partitioning precedent. At MVP volume (tip bursts from live rooms, not card-network scale) one primary is ample. A token wallet also absorbs most micro-volume into internal transfers. Postgres 16 is already deployed.
  - Risks: Hot-row contention if the platform revenue account is updated per transfer. Mitigate with sharded or system accounts and tests for lock ordering.
  - Alternatives: TigerBeetle for the hot transfer path (see separate row, later); Hosted ledgers such as Modern Treasury Ledgers or Formance (adds a vendor whose acceptable-use stance on this category would need confirming)
- **Custom SQL repositories + RawDb client** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Add the following to @bslt/db: (1) withTransaction(fn, {isolation, retries}) that retries on SQLSTATE 40001/40P01 with jitter; (2) SET LOCAL statement_timeout and lock_timeout per transaction, honouring QueryOptions.timeout; (3) skipLocked()/noWait() on the select builder; (4) a typed bigint/minor-units decoder for money columns. Each needs owner approval under the architecture-change rule; none needs new npm packages.
  - Requirement: Safe money movement: no lost updates, bounded lock waits, deterministic retries
  - Rationale: Custom SQL suits a ledger well because explicit SQL, CHECK and deferred constraints and triggers are easier to reason about than ORM output. Four gaps matter for money. QueryOptions.timeout is silently ignored. There is no statement_timeout or lock_timeout. No helper retries serialization or deadlock failures. BIGINT columns come back from postgres.js as strings and need a deliberate decode policy.
  - Risks: Retry helpers must wrap only idempotent closures. External I/O must never run inside a retried transaction; the outbox pattern handles that.
  - Alternatives: Kysely as a typed query builder (new package, against the owner's custom-SQL principle)
- **Double-entry ledger module (generic)** — add-new, foundation-now, L. Optional ledger package (for example main/server/ledger, profile-gated). Tables: ledger_accounts (owner type/id, currency or asset, normal side, allow_negative flag), ledger_transactions (id, idempotency_key UNIQUE, kind, external_ref, created_at), ledger_postings (transaction_id, account_id, amount BIGINT minor units signed or debit/credit, currency, created_at; PK includes created_at so it can be partitioned later), and account_balances (a snapshot updated in the same transaction under FOR UPDATE, with a version column). Enforce sum(postings) = 0 per currency per transaction with a DEFERRABLE constraint trigger. Enforce append-only with a trigger that raises on UPDATE/DELETE plus REVOKE from the app role. Corrections are reversal transactions only. Support pending, posted and voided states for holds (private-show minutes, payout batches). Add property tests for the zero-sum invariant, the no-negative-balance invariant and replay idempotency.
  - Requirement: Creator earnings, platform fee 10–15%, processor fees, refunds, chargebacks, reserves, payouts, crypto, plus auditability for acquirers
  - Rationale: Nothing exists today: billing only mirrors provider subscriptions and invoices. Every money requirement in the plan (splits, wallets, holds, clawbacks, payouts) needs an immutable journal. This is generic enough for any marketplace-style TypeScript platform, so it belongs in the foundation as an optional capability.
  - Risks: Ledger bugs are costly. Needs a daily trial-balance job and reconciliation against processor settlement reports from day one.
  - Alternatives: Formance Ledger (self-hosted OSS); TigerBeetle (later); Hosted ledger SaaS
- **Dedicated ledger engine (TigerBeetle)** — add-new, later, XL. Do not adopt at MVP. Keep the ledger behind a narrow LedgerStore port (createTransfers, lookupBalances, pending/post/void) so TigerBeetle could back the hot path later. Revisit only if sustained ledger writes or hot-account lock waits become the measured bottleneck after sharding system accounts.
  - Requirement: Ledger write throughput at scale
  - Rationale: TigerBeetle's own architecture docs position it beside a general-purpose DB, handling only the hot transfer path. That means two systems of record, cross-store consistency work and new operational load. At MVP volume, with micro-tips netted inside a token wallet, Postgres is sufficient. TigerBeetle is overkill now.
  - Risks: Premature adoption doubles the operational surface and splits balances from metadata.
  - Alternatives: Stay on Postgres with sharded system accounts and partitioned postings
- **BillingService port (provider abstraction)** — augment, foundation-now, L. Keep BillingService for SaaS subscriptions in the foundation, and add a smaller capability-based port alongside it. HostedCheckout.create({amountMinor, currency, reference, recurring?}) returns a redirect or form post. PaymentIngress.verify({rawBody, query, headers, sourceIp}) and parse() return NormalizedPaymentEvent[] with a provider dedupe key synthesized when none exists, plus an optional synchronous response body. Optional Refunds and SubscriptionControl capabilities complete it. Replace the billing_provider ENUM with TEXT plus a code-side registry (or lookup table). Allow N enabled providers, routed by policy (cascade or failover), not one config.provider. Extend the event vocabulary: payment.succeeded (one-time), payment.failed, renewal.succeeded/failed, subscription.expired, refund, chargeback, chargeback_reversed, void, return.
  - Requirement: High-risk hosted-payment-page + postback processors (CCBill, Segpay, Verotel, Epoch), crypto pay-in, multiple processors active at once
  - Rationale: The port assumes Stripe/PayPal semantics: customer and vault APIs, setup intents, product/price admin, one signature header and one active provider. The high-risk processors work differently. CCBill webhooks carry no signature, are verified only by source IP ranges (64.38.212/215/240/241.x), arrive as form-encoded or JSON, and have no event id. Segpay postbacks put parameters in the query string, can use basic auth, and member-management postbacks need a synchronous 'GOOD'/'OK' text reply. Verotel uses a SHA-256 signature over parameters. Epoch reportedly uses an IP allowlist plus an optional HMAC digest. The port also has no one-time or dynamic-amount purchase (PPV, token packs), no void/return/expiration/renewal-failure events, and a Postgres ENUM blocks new providers.
  - Risks: IP-allowlist verification depends on correct trustProxy/X-Forwarded-For handling behind Cloudflare and Caddy, where a misconfiguration lets anyone forge postbacks. Hence the signed-or-IP check plus reconciliation against reporting APIs.
  - Alternatives: Fork-only adapters without changing the foundation port (faster, but every future platform repeats the work)
- **Stripe + PayPal adapters** — keep-with-changes, creator-mvp, S. Keep both adapters in the generic foundation as optional SaaS-billing adapters. Disable them in the creator fork: route all fan payments, including SFW creators, through the high-risk processor stack. Revisit only with counsel and a separate SFW-only entity, brand and domain, and even then expect scrutiny.
  - Requirement: Any payment flow on a platform that hosts suggestive content
  - Rationale: Stripe's restricted-businesses list (updated 2026-09-22) places adult content and services under Prohibited, explicitly naming pay-per-view, adult live-chat features and AI-generated content meeting those criteria. Content-creation platforms are Restricted and need pre-approval. PayPal prohibits sexually oriented digital goods, including website subscriptions and downloadable media. A single merchant entity that also runs an opt-in softcore section is very likely to be judged on its whole business, so even the SFW side cannot safely depend on these accounts.
  - Risks: Account termination and fund holds if the platform runs on Stripe or PayPal while hosting suggestive content.
  - Alternatives: Separate SFW-only legal entity using Stripe Connect (business and legal decision, high termination risk)
- **High-risk card processor adapters** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. CCBill as primary adapter with a second processor (Segpay or Verotel) for cascade and failover, built on the new ingress/checkout port. Ledger accounts per processor: clearing, fees expense, reserve receivable, chargeback-fee expense. A nightly reconciliation job against CCBill DataLink and Segpay SRS. Tables for performer verification, consent records and per-item pre-review decisions, and complaint cases with SLA timestamps, plus a monthly acquirer report export. Confirm in underwriting that each processor accepts AI/nonhuman personas, with a verified human owner per persona. Confirm whether each processor acts as merchant of record for VAT/sales tax.
  - Requirement: Subscriptions, PPV, token packs for tips/private shows/paid messages; suggestive content including AI/nonhuman performers; chargebacks; reserves
  - Rationale: CCBill markets tips and gifts plus live-session tickets to adult and mainstream creator platforms and supports dynamic pricing (enabled by support), upsell/cross-sell one-click events, and refund, chargeback and void webhooks. Segpay offers postbacks, a reporting API (SRS) for reconciliation and a model/affiliate payout platform. Verotel FlexPay uses signed postbacks. Third-party reviews put CCBill adult pricing at about 10.8–14.5% per transaction with a 5% rolling reserve held 26 weeks. That roughly equals the planned 10–15% platform fee, so the fee model must pass processing cost to fans (token-pack pricing) or take it from creator gross. Card-brand rules (Mastercard AN 5196, Visa VIRP with MCC 5967, covering AI-generated adult content) require documented age, identity and consent for everyone depicted, pre-publication review, a complaint process resolved within 7 business days, and monthly flagged-content reports to the acquirer. Those obligations create data-layer requirements.
  - Risks: A high chargeback ratio can get processing terminated; consider velocity limits on token purchases. Reserves tie up 5% of volume for about 6 months, which is a cash-flow load the ledger must show. Processor policy on AI-generated adult content is tightening industry-wide.
  - Alternatives: Epoch (adult specialist; docs only via account team); Specialist ISO/high-risk gateways (CorePay, CommerceGate) with Visa/MC high-integrity-risk registration
- **Token / credit wallet (closed-loop prepaid balance)** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Fan wallet as a ledger liability account denominated in tokens (integer), with a fixed token price per pack stored on the purchase. Spending a token posts fan_wallet -> creator_payable (creator share) + platform_revenue (fee) in one transaction with FOR UPDATE on the fan wallet and a CHECK on the non-negative balance. Private and group shows use a pending hold for N minutes, post actuals per minute or at end, and void the remainder. Paid messages and PPV unlock grant entitlements in the same transaction. Keep tokens closed-loop and non-redeemable for cash to limit money-transmission exposure (needs counsel), and define a breakage and unclaimed-balance policy.
  - Requirement: Live tipping in public rooms, private/group shows, tip menus and goals, paid messages, PPV unlocks
  - Rationale: A hosted-payment-page card transaction per tip is impractical: redirect UX mid-stream, per-transaction fees and chargeback exposure. The cam-style norm is buying token packs (one card transaction, one-click re-purchase via processor upsell flows) and spending them internally as ledger transfers. Tips then become sub-millisecond Postgres transfers, and private-show billing becomes a hold-and-settle pattern.
  - Risks: Stored-value and money-transmitter classification varies by jurisdiction. Chargebacks on token packs after the tokens were spent require clawback from creators (allow a negative creator_payable and offset future earnings).
  - Alternatives: Direct per-item card charges for subscriptions and PPV only, with tokens just for live features
- **Entitlements / access grants** — add-new, foundation-now, M. A generic entitlements table: user_id, resource_type, resource_id, source (subscription | purchase | admin | promo), source_ref, valid_from/valid_until, revoked_at, revoke_reason. Unique partial indexes for active grants. Written in the same transaction as the ledger posting. Creator fork: creator-scoped offers (price, interval, currency) instead of the global plans catalog. Chargebacks revoke all grants and flag the payer.
  - Requirement: Per-creator subscriptions (monthly/annual), PPV posts, recordings and drops, paid messages; revoke on refund or chargeback
  - Rationale: Today access derives from a single active SaaS subscription per user (findActiveByUserId LIMIT 1) and global plan features. A fan needs many concurrent grants scoped to a creator or content item. A generic grant table is reusable across future platforms.
  - Risks: Hot-path checks run on every content view and stream join, so cache in Redis with invalidation on revoke.
  - Alternatives: Extend the subscriptions table with creator_id (keeps PPV and messages without a home)
- **Money type, integer minor units, multi-currency** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Shared Money/Asset contract in main/shared: {amountMinor: bigint (serialized as string), currency}, an ISO-4217 exponent table plus crypto assets (USDC/USDT = 6), Intl.NumberFormat for fiat display, and no floats anywhere. New money columns use BIGINT. Each ledger account holds one currency or asset; FX or crypto conversion is an explicit two-leg transaction through an FX clearing account with the rate stored. Record processor charge currency and settlement currency separately.
  - Requirement: Integer-safe money, multi-currency pricing, USDT/USDC payouts (6 decimals)
  - Rationale: Integer cents are the right instinct. INTEGER caps a row at about $21.4M (fine for line items, not for balances or aggregates). formatPrice hard-codes /100 and 2 decimals. Crypto assets need 6 decimals, so cents-only assumptions break.
  - Risks: JSON number precision loss if bigint money is ever sent as a JS number. Enforce string serialization in the contract layer.
  - Alternatives: NUMERIC(38,0) for crypto base units if amounts may exceed int64 (not needed for 6-decimal stablecoins)
- **Row-level security (tenancy and SFW/softcore separation)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Foundation-now: create a dedicated NOSUPERUSER NOBYPASSRLS app login role that does not own the tables (migrations run as a separate owner role), GRANT authenticated to it, and SET LOCAL ROLE authenticated in applySession, or connect as an authenticated member directly. FORCE RLS on all policy tables. Add a real-DB test proving cross-user reads return 0 rows. Creator fork: a rating column (sfw | suggestive) on creators, posts, media and streams; a session GUC app.content_mode set to 'suggestive' only after age verification plus opt-in; app-level filters on every read path; an RLS backstop policy (rating = 'sfw' OR app_content_mode() = 'suggestive'); separate storage prefixes and rating-checked signed URLs. Ledger and payment tables get no end-user write policies: writes go only through a service role.
  - Requirement: SFW users must never see softcore content; defense in depth for user and tenant data and the ledger
  - Rationale: The policies exist but are not enforced. They are declared TO authenticated, the app never assumes that role, and production connects as the postgres superuser, which bypasses RLS even with FORCE. Content-rating filtering also cuts across feeds, search, recommendations, realtime fan-out and signed media URLs, so RLS alone cannot be the primary control. It is a strong backstop once real.
  - Risks: Turning RLS on will surface queries that silently relied on bypass (workers, admin, migrations). Needs a separate service or bypass role for workers and a staged rollout.
  - Alternatives: Keep app-only enforcement and document RLS as non-enforced (honest, but loses defense in depth)
- **Webhook/postback ingestion, idempotency, reconciliation** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. An inbound payment_inbox table (provider, dedupe_key UNIQUE, raw body/query/headers, source_ip, received_at, status). Insert first with ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING instead of check-then-insert. Process into ledger and entitlements in one transaction. Retry failures from the job queue. Nightly reconciliation pulls processor reports (CCBill DataLink, Segpay SRS) and creates missing transactions or alerts. Generic inbox and reconciliation hooks go in the foundation; processor specifics go in the fork.
  - Requirement: Exactly-once financial effects from at-least-once, possibly missing processor notifications
  - Rationale: Current pattern is sound: event record plus processing in one transaction, a UNIQUE backstop, notifications after commit. Gaps for high-risk processors: no persisted raw inbox before processing (failed events are lost apart from provider retries; Segpay retries only up to 12h, and only if enabled), no synthesized dedupe key for id-less CCBill events, and no reconciliation against processor reporting.
  - Risks: Storing raw payloads keeps payer PII (names, emails, partial card data), so set retention limits and access controls.
  - Alternatives: Keep the current billing_events table and extend its columns
- **Chargebacks, refunds, reserves, payout holds** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Reversal ledger transactions linked to the original. creator_payable may go negative and nets against future earnings. A chargeback_fee expense account. A reserve_receivable account per processor with scheduled release jobs. A creator earnings maturity hold (pending to available after N days, sized to the dispute window) before payout eligibility. Per-processor chargeback and refund ratio metrics with alerting.
  - Requirement: Chargebacks/refunds/reserves with high-risk processors
  - Rationale: The only current handling flips a subscription to canceled. The marketplace needs money-level effects: reverse the sale, claw back creator share, book chargeback fees, and track processor-held reserves that release about 26 weeks later.
  - Risks: Hold periods annoy creators, but the hold length is a business decision, not a technical one.
  - Alternatives: Platform absorbs all chargebacks (simpler, erodes the 10–15% fee quickly)
- **Creator payouts (fiat)** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Tables: payees (KYC status, tax-form status, country), payout_methods, payout_batches/payout_items with a state machine. Ledger flow creator_payable(available) -> payout_clearing -> settled/failed (failure reverses). Executed by idempotent queue jobs. Reuse creator ID verification already required by card-brand performer rules. Collect tax forms and produce annual reporting exports.
  - Requirement: Creator payouts; platform fee 10–15%
  - Rationale: No payee onboarding, payout batching, KYC or tax-form handling exists. Mainstream payout rails restrict adult-adjacent businesses. Adult-friendly options include processor payout platforms (Segpay offers one for models and affiliates) and e-wallets such as Paxum (secondary sources).
  - Risks: Money-transmission and agent-of-payee analysis differs by US state and country, so get counsel before holding creator funds. Payout fraud from account takeover needs step-up auth on payout-method changes.
  - Alternatives: Processor-managed payouts (Segpay payout platform); Paxum e-wallet; Bank wire/SEPA via an adult-tolerant bank partner
- **Crypto payouts (USDC first, USDT optional)** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Phase 1: USDC payouts via a custodial mass-payout provider that accepts this category in writing (NOWPayments markets to adult platforms; confirm its ToS). Platform-side tables: payout_destinations (asset, chain, address, verified_at, sanctions_screen_result), on-chain tx hash, confirmation status. Ledger: a USD creator_payable converted to USDC through an FX clearing transaction at the quoted rate, then to crypto_payout_clearing. Offer USDT only on chains and in jurisdictions where counsel approves. Avoid self-custody hot wallets at MVP.
  - Requirement: Creator payouts including USDT/USDC
  - Rationale: Direct on-chain payouts mean hot-wallet key custody, chain monitoring, gas management, OFAC/sanctions screening of addresses, travel-rule exposure and likely MSB/money-transmitter status. That is a heavy compliance load for an MVP. A custodial or mass-payout provider shifts the wallet operations, though the platform still owns payee KYC and sanctions screening. USDT is restricted on MiCA-regulated EU venues, while USDC is MiCA-authorized. A large adult platform reportedly switched payouts from USDT to USDC for reliability. US GENIUS Act rules take effect by Jan 18, 2027 at the latest and mostly bind issuers, but they signal identity-linked flows.
  - Risks: Provider de-risking (crypto processors also exit adult merchants), address-poisoning or typo losses (require test payouts or address confirmation), and regulatory change in the EU (MiCA) and US (GENIUS Act).
  - Alternatives: Direct on-chain via own wallets (phase-2 at the earliest, with an MSB/licensing review); Circle (USDC) rails, but its acceptable-use stance on this category is unclear and needs written confirmation
- **Crypto pay-in fallback** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Crypto buys token packs only (no direct crypto tips or subscriptions at MVP) through a hosted-invoice provider that accepts the category, settling to USDC. Map it onto the same HostedCheckout/PaymentIngress port. Ledger: crypto_clearing -> fan_wallet on a confirmed payment. Handle under- and over-payment and expiry. Refunds are manual and policy-driven.
  - Requirement: High-risk processing with crypto fallback
  - Rationale: A useful hedge if card processing is suspended, and no chargebacks. Coinbase Commerce prohibits adult content including pay-per-view and adult live chat, and BitPay excludes sexually explicit content and services. Only adult-tolerant crypto gateways fit.
  - Risks: Sanctions and AML exposure on inbound funds (rely on provider screening plus velocity limits), stablecoin depeg risk if balances are held, and gateway de-risking.
  - Alternatives: NOWPayments (markets to adult; confirm ToS); Other adult-tolerant crypto gateways after diligence
- **Read replicas** — keep-with-changes, phase-2, S. Wire an optional read-only RawDb (contextualize returns db plus dbRead). Route only analytics, discovery and reporting repositories to it. Ledger, wallet and entitlement-write paths stay on the primary.
  - Requirement: Scaling discovery, analytics and creator dashboards without loading the ledger primary
  - Rationale: Config exists (DATABASE_READ_REPLICA_URL) but is dead code. Creator analytics and discovery reads are a natural replica workload. Spend decisions and balance checks must never read from a lagging replica.
  - Risks: Replica lag shown as stale earnings in dashboards; label it or read balances from the primary.
  - Alternatives: Materialized views refreshed by the scheduler for creator analytics
- **Partitioning of ledger and event tables** — keep-with-changes, phase-2, M. Design ledger_postings and payment_inbox with created_at in the PK now so they can be partitioned later. Turn on monthly RANGE partitioning with the ensure_*_partition scheduler job once a table passes tens of millions of rows. Detach and archive old partitions for cold storage, subject to retention rules.
  - Requirement: Growth of postings, tip events, stream billing ticks and the payment inbox
  - Rationale: The events table already has a working monthly-partition pattern. Ledger postings and tip events are append-only and time-ordered, a natural fit. Balances come from the snapshot table, so partitioning postings doesn't slow balance reads.
  - Risks: Global UNIQUE idempotency keys cannot span partitions unless the partition key is in the key, so keep idempotency keys in an unpartitioned table.
  - Alternatives: BRIN indexes on created_at without partitioning (cheaper interim step)
- **Postgres job queue + scheduler (outbox)** — keep, foundation-now, S. Keep. Adopt the convention that any external side effect (payout API call, email, crypto send) is a job enqueued inside the ledger transaction with an idempotency key derived from the ledger transaction id.
  - Requirement: Payout execution, reserve releases, reconciliation, partition maintenance, post-commit side effects
  - Rationale: It lives in the same DB, so jobs can be enqueued atomically with ledger postings (transactional outbox). It already has SKIP LOCKED, backoff retries and idempotency keys. No Redis or Kafka is needed for money workflows at MVP.
  - Risks: Queue table bloat at high volume; vacuum tuning and partitioning or archiving of completed jobs.
  - Alternatives: pg-boss/Graphile Worker (new packages; not needed)
- **Append-only enforcement (audit + ledger)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE triggers that RAISE on ledger_transactions, ledger_postings and audit_events. REVOKE UPDATE and DELETE from the app role. Corrections are reversal entries only. Optional hash-chaining of ledger transactions for tamper evidence.
  - Requirement: Immutable financial and audit records for acquirer and card-brand audits and dispute evidence
  - Rationale: audit_events is append-only by comment only. The same must not hold for the ledger. Enforcement needs a non-superuser app role, which ties into the RLS fix.
  - Risks: Migrations or backfills need an owner-role path. Document a break-glass procedure.
  - Alternatives: Rely on code review only
- **Connection pooling (PgBouncer)** — keep-with-changes, phase-2, S. Give pubsub its own direct connection and move API traffic to transaction pooling. Pin the PgBouncer image version. Size pools for wallet-transfer bursts.
  - Requirement: Bursty live-event traffic (tip storms) without exhausting connections
  - Rationale: Session pooling was chosen only because pubsub uses LISTEN/NOTIFY. SET LOCAL-based session context works under transaction pooling. The image is pinned to :latest.
  - Risks: Prepared statements in transaction mode need driver settings (postgres.js prepare: false or PgBouncer 1.21+ prepared-statement support).
  - Alternatives: Keep session mode and raise pool size (works at small scale)

### Gaps

- No ledger, wallet, balance, fee split, reserve or payout model exists anywhere in the repo. Every money requirement for the creator platform is net-new.
- No one-time or dynamic-amount purchase or order model for PPV, token packs, paid messages or private-show tickets. Checkout is plan-subscription only.
- No multi-processor routing or cascade. The factory activates exactly one provider, and billing_provider is a 2-value Postgres ENUM.
- No processor reconciliation against settlement and reporting APIs (CCBill DataLink, Segpay SRS) and no daily trial balance.
- No payee onboarding: creator KYC, tax-form collection, annual tax reporting exports, sanctions/OFAC screening of payees and crypto addresses.
- No card-brand compliance records required by Mastercard AN 5196 / Visa VIRP: performer age/ID and consent records (including the verified human owner of AI personas), per-item pre-publication review decisions, complaint and takedown cases with the 7-business-day SLA, and a monthly acquirer report export.
- No fraud and risk controls on money flows: velocity limits on token purchases, per-processor chargeback and refund ratio monitoring, step-up auth for payout-method changes.
- Money formatting and types assume 2-decimal fiat. No currency exponent table, no crypto asset support, no shared Money contract, and INTEGER (not BIGINT) amounts.
- RLS is not enforced at runtime: policies are TO authenticated, the app never assumes the role, and production connects as the postgres superuser. There is also no content-rating model or session content_mode for SFW/softcore separation.
- DB client ignores QueryOptions.timeout and sets no statement_timeout or lock_timeout. No retry helper for serialization or deadlock failures.
- Read replica config is parsed but unused.
- Unresolved business and legal inputs that shape the schema: whether each processor acts as merchant of record (VAT/sales tax), the money-transmitter / agent-of-payee position for holding creator funds and closed-loop tokens, token breakage and unclaimed-balance (escheat) policy, and how the 10–15% fee coexists with ~10.8–14.5% adult processing plus a 5%/26-week reserve (fees on top vs deducted from creator gross).
- New npm packages and architectural changes (ledger package, new payment port, role and RLS changes) need owner approval. All items above are recommendations, not decisions.

## Infrastructure, hosting, operations and runtime (read-only review of infra/, .github/workflows, docs/deploy, main/apps/server-rust and the server runtime wiring, plus vendor terms researched on 2026-11-10). Bottom line: the code-level runtime choices hold up for this product: Node 24/TypeScript, Fastify, Postgres with a SKIP LOCKED queue, the S3-compatible storage adapter, Cloudflare at the edge, and portable Docker images. The deployment topology does not. It is one VM running docker compose, with Postgres, Redis, API, WebSockets, workers and uploads on one host. Every deploy runs `compose down`, which takes the whole stack down, database included. Backups need a database reachable from GitHub runners. Terraform state is kept as a CI artifact. The only realtime fan-out is Postgres NOTIFY. None of that survives live rooms, heavy media egress or a privacy-sensitive user base. The fix is to evolve the stack, not rewrite it: add process roles, zero-downtime deploys, managed HA Postgres with PITR, Redis/Valkey as a required live-room bus, buy live video from a vendor, serve media from object storage behind a CDN with signed URLs, and set up real observability and secrets handling. Choose hosting by acceptable-use terms, and make sure the core app host never stores or serves softcore media bytes. Every new package or architectural change below is a recommendation for the owner to approve, not a decision.

### Verdicts

- **Cloud account / blast-radius isolation** — add-new, creator-mvp, S. Give the creator platform its own cloud account or project, its own Cloudflare account or zone, its own container registry namespace and its own GitHub environment secrets. Never deploy it to the shared droplet.
  - Requirement: Legal suggestive content, payment processing and privacy-sensitive users must not share fate with unrelated products
  - Rationale: Today one DigitalOcean account and one Caddyfile front ganbate.io, kanustudio.com, the bslt.dev storefront (Stripe) and CI. If a provider took a trust-and-safety action over adult-adjacent content, or one tenant were compromised, all of them would go down together, and processor or KYC reviews look at shared infrastructure too.
  - Risks: Slightly more billing and admin overhead.
  - Alternatives: Separate projects in the same DO team (weaker isolation)
- **Primary compute hosting provider (currently a DigitalOcean droplet; GCP module unused)** — keep-with-changes, creator-mvp, M. Creator MVP: stay on DigitalOcean (cheapest operations for a solo team; bandwidth allowances; managed PG and Valkey), in a dedicated account, only after written pre-clearance from DO trust and safety that age-gated, non-explicit suggestive content is acceptable. Make sure no softcore media is stored on or served from DO. If DO declines or hedges, use AWS (ECS/EC2 plus RDS plus ElastiCache) for the core app, keeping media off AWS egress.
  - Requirement: Host a legal suggestive-content platform cost-effectively without deplatforming risk
  - Rationale: DO's AUP (updated 2026-03-20) bans only unlawful pornography: CSAM, depictions without consent and NCII deepfakes. Its ToS (updated 2026-08-22) section 5.6 withdraws free bandwidth from accounts hosting 'content of an adult or pornographic nature', and DO keeps sole-discretion suspension rights. Lawful softcore is not banned, but enforcement is discretionary. AWS's AUP has no general adult-content ban. Hetzner's terms ban 'pornographic or obscene material ... or content that offends common decency', so it is excluded. Google Cloud's AUP has no general adult ban, but its GenAI policy bans sexual-gratification content, and the GCP module is an unused single VM. The key design move: if all softcore media bytes and live video live with a media/CDN vendor chosen for its policy, the core host only stores metadata and is far less policy-exposed and easier to move.
  - Risks: Pre-clearance can be revoked. Keep images, Terraform and data portable, so a forced move takes days rather than months.
  - Alternatives: AWS as primary from day one (clearest AUP, highest egress cost); OVHcloud or bare metal with unmetered bandwidth (terms unclear; operations-heavy; better as phase-2 cost play); Hetzner (excluded by ToS for softcore; acceptable only for SFW-only auxiliary workloads)
- **Terraform modules and state** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Foundation: a remote backend with locking and encryption (S3-compatible on DO Spaces, R2 or S3, or OpenTofu with state encryption, or HCP Terraform). Keep DO as the single reference module and add a VPC, a load balancer, managed PG (node_count >= 2 for prod), managed Valkey, a Spaces/R2 bucket and a firewall that only admits Cloudflare ranges on 80/443. SSH should default to closed or a VPN/bastion CIDR. Freeze the GCP module as an example, or move it to docs. Creator fork: separate staging and production workspaces in the dedicated account.
  - Requirement: Repeatable, auditable infrastructure for multiple environments; secrets not leaked through state
  - Rationale: State is a CI artifact: no locking, it expires, and it holds sensitive values. The modules are single-VM stubs, both commented out. Two clouds at stub depth is breadth without depth, so one well-built reference module serves the foundation better.
  - Risks: Migrating existing state from the artifact needs care. Do a one-time import.
  - Alternatives: Pulumi (TypeScript) — new tooling, needs owner approval; Keep Terraform but adopt OpenTofu for native state encryption
- **Process roles (HTTP API, WebSocket gateway, workers/cron and media run in one Node process)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Same image, multiple entrypoints or a PROCESS_ROLE (api | realtime | worker | media-worker) selected at boot. Profiles already gate the capabilities, so this extends an existing pattern. Add SIGTERM drain for WebSockets (stop accepting, send a close code with a reconnect hint, wait N seconds). Cron is already leader-elected, so it is safe to run on several worker replicas.
  - Requirement: Horizontal scaling of the API and WebSocket tiers, and isolating CPU-heavy work from latency-sensitive paths
  - Rationale: Live rooms produce many long-lived sockets. Transcoding and thumbnailing burn CPU. Payouts and webhooks need reliable workers. These have different scaling curves and deploy cadences. Running them in one process means a transcode spike stalls tip acknowledgements, and every API deploy disconnects every viewer.
  - Risks: Health checks and metrics must be role-aware, and the Docker smoke tests must cover each role.
  - Alternatives: Separate apps/packages per role (more structure, more boilerplate)
- **Deploy pipeline (`compose down` then up over SSH; Postgres in the app compose)** — replace, foundation-now, M. Foundation: rolling or blue-green deploys of stateless roles only (never restart the database in an app deploy), health-gated, with automatic rollback. Remove databases from the production app compose (keep them in dev compose). Creator MVP: two or more app nodes behind a load balancer with WebSocket support and connection draining. Tool choice is the owner's: Kamal 2 (Docker over SSH, built-in proxy, no npm dependency), Docker Swarm (already in Docker), or managed Kubernetes (DOKS/EKS) once there are more than about 5 services.
  - Requirement: Zero-downtime deploys while streams, tips and private shows are live
  - Rationale: Every deploy currently stops all containers, Postgres included, and drops every socket. That is incompatible with paid live sessions, and an interrupted private show turns directly into refunds.
  - Risks: Kubernetes is overkill for a solo team at MVP. Prefer the simplest tool that does rolling updates plus draining.
  - Alternatives: Kamal 2; Docker Swarm; DOKS / EKS; ECS Fargate (if AWS)
- **PostgreSQL hosting** — replace, creator-mvp, M. Managed Postgres with HA standby and PITR (DO Managed PostgreSQL with 2+ nodes, or RDS Multi-AZ if on AWS). Standardise on PG 17 across CI, compose and backups (today it is 16 vs 17). Add a read replica later for analytics and creator dashboards. Encryption at rest is provided; enforce TLS (DB_SSL=true). Foundation: make managed PG the documented production default and keep self-run for dev and the demo.
  - Requirement: Money-moving ledger (subscriptions, PPV, tips, payouts) needs durability, PITR and HA; privacy-sensitive data
  - Rationale: The self-run postgres:16 container on the app host, with nightly logical dumps, gives up to 24h of data loss and single-host failure. That is unacceptable once tips and payouts are recorded. The custom SQL, RLS via SET LOCAL and SKIP LOCKED queue all work unchanged on managed Postgres.
  - Risks: Managed connection limits mean a pooler is still needed. Major-version upgrades need a rehearsal.
  - Alternatives: Self-run on bare metal with Patroni plus pgBackRest (phase-2 cost play; needs DBA time); Crunchy Bridge / Aiven (check their AUPs)
- **PgBouncer (bitnami/pgbouncer:latest, session mode)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Pin a maintained PgBouncer image (or use the managed provider's pooler). Move the LISTEN connection to a dedicated direct DSN and switch app traffic to transaction pooling, with PgBouncer 1.21+ for protocol-level prepared statements. Verify postgres.js prepare settings in tests.
  - Requirement: Connection scaling for multiple API/worker replicas
  - Rationale: Bitnami moved its versioned free images to the unmaintained 'bitnamilegacy' registry around 2025-08-28 (secondary sources and a GitLab MR), so ':latest' from docker.io/bitnami is unsupported and unpinned. Session mode pins one server connection per client, so it pools almost nothing. SET LOCAL (RLS) is transaction-scoped, so it is compatible with transaction pooling. Only LISTEN needs a direct connection.
  - Risks: Transaction pooling breaks any session-level SET or advisory lock. Audit for these before switching.
  - Alternatives: Managed pooler (DO connection pools, RDS Proxy); pgcat / Supavisor
- **Backups (GitHub-runner pg_dump)** — replace, foundation-now, S. Creator MVP: managed PITR (7-30 days) as the primary, plus weekly encrypted logical dumps (age/GPG) taken inside the private network and sent to a bucket in a separate account with object lock. Quarterly restore drills (already documented). Foundation: retire the runner-based workflow, or gate it behind a self-hosted in-VPC runner, and add encryption.
  - Requirement: Privacy-sensitive data, financial records, recoverability
  - Rationale: Dumping from GitHub-hosted runners means exposing the production database to the internet. Dumps are unencrypted client-side, and nightly logical dumps are the only recovery point.
  - Risks: Backups contain PII and KYC references. Restrict key access and set retention aligned with deletion requests.
  - Alternatives: WAL-G or pgBackRest to S3/R2 for self-run Postgres
- **Redis (optional cache and rate limit)** — keep-with-changes, creator-mvp, S. Make Redis/Valkey required in the creator fork: managed (DO Managed Caching for Valkey or ElastiCache), private network, TLS. Keep it optional in the foundation.
  - Requirement: Live rooms: presence and viewer lists, ephemeral counters, distributed rate limits, fan-out
  - Rationale: ioredis is already a dependency, and Redis is the cheapest fit for the high-frequency ephemeral state live rooms generate. That state should not go into Postgres.
  - Risks: Do not treat Redis as durable for money. Tips must commit to Postgres first.
  - Alternatives: Self-run Valkey with replica (cheaper, more operations)
- **Realtime fan-out (Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY invalidations)** — augment, foundation-now, M. Foundation: define a PubSub port with the existing Postgres adapter plus a Redis adapter (ioredis already present), selected by env. Creator MVP: a dedicated realtime role on Redis pub/sub for room events. Tip and paid events commit to Postgres, then an outbox publishes them to the room. Phase 2, if load tests show limits: Centrifugo (an off-the-shelf Go server with presence, history and Redis/NATS scaling) or NATS.
  - Requirement: Public rooms with tipping, goals, tip menus, viewer lists and chat across many WebSocket nodes
  - Rationale: NOTIFY suits low-rate record-version invalidation, and that is what the code does ({key, version}, then clients refetch). Live rooms need high-rate fan-out of small events to thousands of sockets per room. NOTIFY serialises on commit, is capped at an 8 KB payload, and every event would hit the database. It also forces session pooling.
  - Risks: Two fan-out paths to reason about. Keep record-sync on Postgres and room events on Redis, and document which is which.
  - Alternatives: Centrifugo; NATS; Managed realtime (Ably/Pusher; check AUPs, adds per-message cost)
- **Job queue (Postgres SKIP LOCKED + cron)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Keep. Run it in the separate worker role. Add per-queue concurrency (media vs payouts vs email), visible dead-letter handling, completed-row retention or partitioning, and queue depth and oldest-job-age metrics with alerts. Never route chat, tips display or presence through it. Revisit (pg-boss, NATS JetStream) only if sustained throughput or contention shows up in metrics.
  - Requirement: Background jobs: transcode orchestration, payouts, webhooks, emails, analytics rollups
  - Rationale: A transactional Postgres queue gives exactly-once-enqueue with business writes, the outbox semantics the payment and payout flows need. Redis/BullMQ would lose that. SKIP LOCKED comfortably handles hundreds of jobs per second with good indexes and cleanup.
  - Risks: Table bloat without vacuum or retention tuning.
  - Alternatives: pg-boss (new dependency); NATS JetStream; BullMQ (rejected for money flows: Redis durability)
- **Live video infrastructure (none today)** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. A LiveVideoProvider port in the creator fork. Public rooms use managed RTMPS/SRT/WHIP ingest with LL-HLS or WebRTC delivery and recording-to-VOD (candidates: Cloudflare Stream Live at $1/1,000 min delivered and $5/1,000 min stored, or AWS IVS). Private and group shows use a WebRTC SFU (LiveKit Cloud or IVS Real-Time). Get written AUP pre-clearance before integrating. Phase 2: self-hosted LiveKit OSS on high-bandwidth servers for cost. Final vendor choice belongs to the media/video lens.
  - Requirement: Public rooms, private and group shows, recordings into PPV, mobile and desktop web
  - Rationale: Nothing in the stack ingests, transcodes or delivers live video, and building an SFU/CDN pipeline is a team-sized effort with huge egress. Buy it for the MVP, behind a provider port so it can move. Vendor terms differ. Cloudflare Stream and Realtime can remove content it judges 'illegal or harmful'. Mux bars content it 'reasonably deems ... obscene, or otherwise objectionable'. LiveKit Cloud bars 'content that may be harmful to minors', so it needs a hard age gate. AWS's AUP has no general adult ban.
  - Risks: Vendor policy reversal is the largest platform risk. Keep the recording copy in your own bucket so you can switch delivery vendors.
  - Alternatives: Cloudflare Stream Live; AWS IVS (low-latency + real-time stages); LiveKit Cloud / self-hosted LiveKit; Mux (policy is discretionary)
- **Media storage and delivery (S3 adapter; production default is a local volume served through the API)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Foundation: object storage as the production default, and stop hardcoding STORAGE_PROVIDER=local in the deploy workflow. Creator MVP: R2 or bunny.net storage behind a CDN. Use separate buckets and separate delivery hostnames (ideally a separate domain) for SFW and softcore. Short-lived signed URLs or tokens for all paid and softcore objects. Cache keys that never cross the SFW/softcore boundary. No public bucket listing. Originals in a private bucket.
  - Requirement: Heavy media egress, PPV and softcore gating, SFW users never seeing softcore
  - Rationale: The S3-compatible adapter with presigning is the right abstraction. Serving /uploads from a local volume through the API blocks horizontal scaling and puts media bytes on the core host. Egress dominates cost: R2 has $0 egress ($0.015/GB-month standard storage).
  - Risks: Concentrating edge, CDN, Stream and R2 all on Cloudflare means one 'sole judgment' decision can take everything down. Keep a replicated copy in a second provider.
  - Alternatives: bunny.net Storage + CDN (cheap egress; AUP not verified); Backblaze B2 + CDN; S3 + CloudFront (egress cost)
- **Media processing (ffmpeg wrapper + sharp)** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Foundation: a dedicated media-worker image or role with a pinned ffmpeg, and media dist packaging asserted in the Dockerfile like realtime and workers. Creator MVP: push live and VOD transcoding to the video vendor, and keep local ffmpeg/sharp for images, thumbnails, blur previews and watermark overlays, scaled by queue depth.
  - Requirement: Thumbnails, previews, blurred teasers, recording post-processing

### Verdicts

- **Live video media plane (SFU)** — add-new, creator-mvp, L. Self-hosted LiveKit (open-source Go SFU) on DigitalOcean or another adult-tolerant host, with Redis (required for multi-node), LiveKit Ingress and LiveKit Egress. Use WebRTC for all MVP rooms (public, group, private), with simulcast layers. Cap free and anonymous viewers at 480p/720p. Use the embedded TURN over TLS:443. Put everything behind a LiveVideoProvider port in server/core (createRoom, mintPublisherToken, mintViewerToken, revokeParticipant, startRecording, verifyWebhook), the same way billing is a port, so IVS can be a fallback adapter. Build it as an optional capability package (for example main/server/live) so it can be upstreamed to the foundation later. New npm packages (livekit-server-sdk, livekit-client) need owner approval.
  - Requirement: Public live rooms with tipping, private and group shows, mobile and desktop web, latency low enough for the tip-and-reaction loop
  - Rationale: The repo has no live video. Latency: a WebRTC SFU delivers about 0.2-0.5 s glass-to-glass. LL-HLS is 3-7 s: Mux documents 4-7 s for LL-HLS, and AWS documents IVS Low-Latency stream-start latency of about 3-4 s. That is tolerable for large public rooms but not for two-way private or group shows. Cost: delivery is the biggest variable cost, and non-tipping viewers dominate viewer-hours. Assumptions: 720p at about 2 Mbps, so about 0.9 GB per viewer-hour. Estimated cost per viewer-hour by option: (1) Self-hosted LiveKit on DigitalOcean: about $0.01-0.02 (overage $0.01/GiB, pooled allowance, plus compute). (2) Cloudflare Realtime SFU: about $0.045 ($0.05/GB after 1 TB free). (3) Mux LL-HLS: $0.048 at 720p to $0.06 at 1080p, plus $1.50-1.88 per broadcast-hour of input. (4) Cloudflare Stream: $0.06. (5) IVS Low-Latency: $0.072 HD or $0.144 Full HD at first-tier North America rates, plus $0.20-2.00 per hour of input. (6) IVS Real-Time: $0.072 per participant-hour. (7) LiveKit Cloud: about $0.11-0.14 ($0.0004-0.0005 per participant-minute plus $0.10-0.12/GB). At 1M viewer-hours a month that is roughly $10-20k self-hosted against $45-140k managed. At a 12.5% take, $0.06 per viewer-hour needs about $0.48 of GMV per viewer-hour just to cover video. Acceptable-use terms: LiveKit Cloud, Mux, Agora and Dolby keep discretionary 'obscene', 'objectionable' or 'pornographic' clauses. The AWS and DigitalOcean policies do not ban lawful adult content. Self-hosting the open-source SFU removes media-plane termination risk and fits the clone-and-own ethos. The LiveKit Node server SDK keeps room logic in server/core.
  - Risks: Ops burden: UDP port ranges, TURN, autoscaling, and node failure during a paid show. A LiveKit room must fit on one node, so the largest public room is capped by one node's network bandwidth. DigitalOcean droplet bandwidth caps. Needs load testing before launch and on-call coverage for paid shows. If the SFU is hosted on GCP instead, its acceptable-use policy must be verified first.
  - Alternatives: AWS IVS Real-Time (WHIP ingest, 12 publishers, 10k subscribers by default, raisable to 25k, 720p ingest cap) plus IVS Low-Latency for large rooms: managed, with permissive acceptable-use terms, at about 5-7x the self-hosted cost; Cloudflare Realtime SFU: cheap egress, but low-level (you build signaling and recording yourself), and Stream's WHIP inputs cannot be recorded; LiveKit Cloud: for development and staging, or SFW-only, unless LiveKit confirms in writing that age-gated suggestive content is acceptable; mediasoup: a Node-embedded SFU with more control but much more to build (signaling, recording, ingest); Ant Media Server Enterprise: per-instance licensing ($89-109 per instance per month), Java
- **LL-HLS overflow for very large public rooms** — add-new, phase-2, L. When a room exceeds about 1-2k viewers, or for anonymous viewers, LiveKit Egress produces HLS/LL-HLS segments to object storage plus a CDN, or sends RTMP egress to IVS Low-Latency or Mux.   - Requirement: Chat, tip events, presence and viewer lists at live-room scale; mobile and desktop web
  - Rationale: Node with ws is fine. The core is sound (noServer mode, frame cap, heartbeat that terminates dead sockets, one serialization per publish), and fan-out of small messages is I/O-bound, which Node handles well into the tens of thousands of sockets per process. No other runtime is warranted for the MVP. The Rust app is a loopback dev auth backend, not a realtime tier, and should not be repurposed. Real gaps: (1) it shares the API process, so API deploys drop sockets and the two cannot scale separately; (2) no backpressure or slow-consumer handling; (3) no subscription or message-rate caps per socket; (4) no anonymous read-only mode; (5) the JWT is checked only at upgrade and never re-validated; (6) no server-initiated kick; (7) the JWT travels as a subprotocol, which ws's default handleProtocols echoes back in the response header.
  - Risks: A single Node event loop gets GC pauses under very large fan-out; mitigate with horizontal gateway instances.
  - Alternatives: Centrifugo (Go, open source) as a sidecar with Redis or NATS engines, built-in presence, history and recovery, and backend proxying, if the gateway exceeds about 100k concurrent sockets; uWebSockets.js (faster, but non-standard distribution, so owner approval needed); Ably (managed; its acceptable-use policy has no adult clause, but its 'harmful' wording is open-ended)
- **Realtime protocol: record-sync and optimistic locking vs. live events** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Keep the invalidation bus and record-sync for low-rate CRUD caches (record-sync is effectively unused, with only the 'users' table registered; consider pruning it). Add a second message class: typed, payload-carrying channel events (chat.message, tip.received, goal.progress, room.state, presence.count, moderation.delete), defined as checked contracts in main/shared. Give each channel monotonically increasing sequence numbers and a bounded per-channel history (Redis Stream or capped list) for gap-free reconnect recovery. Tips go durably to the ledger first, in a Postgres transaction with an idempotency key, and are published after commit. Compute goal totals with an atomic UPDATE ... SET total = total + $1 RETURNING, or Redis INCRBY, and broadcast them throttled.
  - Requirement: Chat, tip events, goals and tip menus, presence, viewer lists
  - Rationale: This design does not fit live interaction. Chat, tips and presence are append-only events or ephemeral state, so there are no write conflicts for optimistic locking to resolve. Invalidate-then-refetch multiplies load: a 2,000-viewer room at 5 chat messages per second would trigger about 10,000 HTTP refetches per second. A goal-progress row updated by many concurrent tippers under version checks produces a storm of 409 responses. The single 1,000-entry history ring across all keys means the reconnect-replay window shrinks to seconds under chat load, and per-node timestamps break replay across nodes. The design does suit low-rate creator-owned records: tip-menu and goal definitions, room settings, schedules, notifications.
  - Risks: Two message classes add client complexity; keep a single socket and a single router on the client.
  - Alternatives: Centrifugo channels with history and recovery instead of a custom per-channel history
- **Channel authorization model** — replace, foundation-now, M. Make it deny-by-default. Each channel namespace registers a server/core authorizer, or the client presents a server-signed channel grant (HMAC or JWT with channel, expiry and read/publish capabilities) minted in the same step as the media token. Re-check on refresh, and push revocations through the propagation module.
  - Requirement: Paid and private show chat, creator-only viewer lists, SFW/softcore separation
  - Rationale: canSubscribeToKey is a deny-list that protects only notifications. Every other key, including the per-user list:{userId}:* keys, is readable by any logged-in socket. With payload-carrying events, this would expose paid-show chat and softcore-room activity.
  - Risks: Migrating the existing notifications channel (small).
  - Alternatives: Centrifugo channel tokens or a subscribe proxy
- **Cross-node fan-out (Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY)** — augment, foundation-now, S. Generalize the shared 'PostgresPubSub' type into a PubSubAdapter interface. Keep Postgres as the foundation default so a small app needs no Redis. Add a Redis pub/sub adapter (ioredis is already a dependency; use sharded pub/sub on a cluster). In the creator fork, make Redis required and use it for fan-out, presence, per-channel history and rate limits. Start the Postgres listener only when realtime is enabled.
  - Requirement: Multi-node chat, tip and presence fan-out at live-room message rates
  - Rationale: LISTEN/NOTIFY is fine for low-rate invalidations, but not for chat and tip fan-out, for five reasons: (1) NOTIFY takes a global lock at commit that serializes notifying transactions (the Recall.ai incident); (2) the payload is capped at 8,000 bytes; (3) LISTEN needs a direct or session-mode connection, not PgBouncer transaction pooling, which matters on managed Postgres; (4) it loads the primary database that holds the money ledger; (5) delivery is fire-and-forget. Redis is already an optional dependency, and self-hosted LiveKit requires it anyway.
  - Risks: Redis becomes a hard dependency of the creator fork and needs high availability (managed Redis or Sentinel).
  - Alternatives: NATS or JetStream (later: multi-region, durable streams); Centrifugo's Redis or NATS engine
- **Presence and viewer lists** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Redis presence per room: a ZSET of connection or user IDs scored by last heartbeat, swept after about 45 s, counted with ZCARD. Broadcast counts throttled to every 2-5 s instead of on each join or leave. A paginated viewer list visible only to the creator and moderators, with tippers first. Anonymous viewers are counted but not listed, and viewers in softcore rooms can hide themselves. Use LiveKit participant webhooks only for private-show presence and billing start and stop. A small generic presence primitive goes in the foundation; room semantics go in the creator fork.
  - Requirement: Viewer lists, viewer counts, goals visibility, creator analytics
  - Rationale: There is no presence primitive today. Public viewer counts must include anonymous viewers and any future HLS viewers, who do not appear in the SFU's participant list. Viewer identity in softcore rooms is privacy-sensitive.
  - Risks: Heartbeat write load at scale; batch heartbeats per gateway node.
  - Alternatives: Centrifugo presence and presence_stats
- **Chat persistence, moderation hooks and rate limits** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. The send path goes through server/core, over HTTP POST or a WebSocket publish. Check in order: auth, mute, ban and slow mode, a Redis token bucket per user and room, and a synchronous keyword and link filter. Then persist append-only to Postgres (time-partitioned) and publish the payload event. Asynchronous ML moderation can issue retroactive moderation.delete events. Tip and paid-message events are only ever authored by the server, after the ledger commit. Frame snapshots from the live video (egress image output or thumbnails) feed the moderation queue.
  - Requirement: Chat in public and private rooms, paid messages, automated and human moderation
  - Rationale: No chat exists. Live chat needs synchronous gates (bans, slow mode, filters), durable storage for moderation and dispute evidence, and deletion events that reach every connected client.
  - Risks: Chat table growth (partition and expire). Moderation latency on fast rooms.
  - Alternatives: Managed chat SaaS (check each vendor's acceptable-use terms)
- **Edge CSP and Permissions-Policy** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Generate the CSP and Permissions-Policy from the enabled capability profile, keeping the audit as a check on the generated output. The live capability adds media-src 'self' blob: <cdn>; connect-src https://<sfu> wss://<sfu> <cdn>; worker-src blob:; and camera=(self) microphone=(self), ideally only on broadcast routes.
  - Requirement: Browser broadcasting, WebRTC and HLS playback from vendor or SFU origins
  - Rationale: Caddy sends camera=() and microphone=(). EDGE_CSP has no media-src or worker-src and allows connect-src only to 'self' wss: Stripe and Sentry. Browser publishing and blob: MSE playback would both be blocked, and hls.js workers would be blocked under script-src. The byte-identical audit means these headers are hand-copied into four places.
  - Risks: Loosening the CSP too broadly; keep origins explicit.
  - Alternatives: Hand-edit the four copies in the fork
- **Video webhooks and orchestration on the existing Postgres queue** — keep, creator-mvp, S. Verify LiveKit (or IVS) webhook signatures and enqueue idempotently by event ID: room_started, room_finished, participant_joined, participant_left, egress_ended. Billing ticks and recording publication run as jobs.
  - Requirement: Recording-to-PPV automation, per-minute billing start and stop, room lifecycle
  - Rationale: The SKIP LOCKED queue with 1 s polling and leader-elected cron already exists and handles MVP-scale webhook and tick volumes.
  - Risks: Queue table bloat under high tick volume; needs vacuum and retention policies.
- **Web player and broadcaster UI (mobile and desktop)** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. React player and broadcaster components wrapping livekit-client (adaptive stream, simulcast layer selection, 480p default on cellular, picture-in-picture), plus overlays for tips, goals and the tip menu driven by realtime events. hls.js for phase-2 overflow. Both are new npm dependencies and need owner approval.
  - Requirement: Mobile and desktop web, no native apps
  - Rationale: React stays a renderer: SFU and token logic lives in server/core and shared, and player components live in main/client/ui. Mobile web constraints: autoplay must be muted, backgrounding and screen lock stop broadcasts, and cellular data needs a data-saver default.
  - Risks: iOS Safari WebRTC quirks; test on real devices.
  - Alternatives: Vendor web players (IVS player SDK, Mux Player)

### Gaps

- No live video at all: no SFU/WebRTC, RTMP/WHIP ingest, LL-HLS or live recording anywhere in the repo.
- No presence, viewer counts or viewer lists.
- The realtime protocol cannot carry payloads (invalidation only), so chat, tips and goals have no event channel; there is no per-channel sequence or history for gap-free reconnect.
- Subscription authorization is open by default; there are no paid or private channel grants, no SFW/softcore channel separation, and the revocation module is not wired.
- No anonymous or guest realtime connections for logged-out public-room viewers.
- No WebSocket backpressure, slow-consumer handling, per-socket subscription caps or inbound rate limits; no WebSocket fan-out load test.
- No TURN infrastructure for restrictive mobile and corporate networks.
- No production VOD packaging for PPV recordings (ABR ladder, encryption keys behind an entitlement check, signed CDN delivery with short TTLs); createHLSStream is a stub.
- No wallet or ledger to settle per-minute show ticks and tips that realtime events depend on (owned by the payments lens, but it blocks live features).
- No chat storage, moderation tooling (slow mode, bans, deletion events) or live-frame sampling for moderation.
- No media quality-of-experience or SFU observability (bitrate, packet loss, join time, rebuffering) in the Prometheus setup.
- The CSP and Permissions-Policy are hard-coded and block camera, microphone, blob: media and vendor origins; they are not composable per capability.
- No video-provider abstraction (port), so a vendor termination for content-policy reasons would force a rewrite.
- The WebSocket gateway is not deployable separately from the API; API deploys drop every live room's sockets.

### Verdicts

- **Core client stack (React 19 + Vite 8 SPA, @bslt/ui tokens, custom router/query, generated client/hooks)** — keep, foundation-now, S. Keep React, Vite, @bslt/ui, the custom router and query layer, and the generated client and hooks as the app runtime. Do not migrate to Next.js. Add the public-page rendering described below beside the SPA, not in place of it.
  - Requirement: Creator dashboards, paywalls, live room UI and fast mobile web
  - Rationale: Dashboards, checkout, messages and live rooms are authenticated, per-user and interactive, which is what an SPA is good at. The stack is current. The generated client already takes fetchImpl and baseUrl, so the same contracts can run in a Worker or Node renderer, and that is the key enabler for adding public-page rendering without a framework swap. The gaps are in public-page rendering and media, not in the SPA itself.
  - Risks: The custom router has no loaders or data APIs, so request-time rendering needs a small route-level load() contract (next verdict).
  - Alternatives: Next.js (App Router/RSC): wants to own routing, data fetching and the server, so it would duplicate Fastify and the custom contracts. It contradicts 'frameworks are renderers' and implies a large rewrite.; TanStack Start 1.0 (selective per-route SSR): technically strong, but replaces the custom router and query layer.; React Router v7 framework mode (ssr:false + prerender, or full SSR): replaces the custom router.
- **Public page renderer seam (generalize the build-time prerenderer to request-time rendering + hydration)** — augment, foundation-now, L. Define a framework-free PublicRoute contract: match(path) → load(params, apiClient with fetchImpl) returning data or notFound → head(data) returning tags → Component. Use it for three modes: build-time (today's pages), on-demand (a job that re-renders on publish) and per-request (streamed with react-dom/server renderToReadableStream). Add QueryCache dehydrate/hydrate: serialize the loaded queries into a JSON script tag and seed them with setQueryData. Render public routes inside a dedicated public shell that is identical on server and client, so hydrateRoot works. Mark which API routes are public and cacheable in the route contract; the `public` flag already exists.
  - Requirement: Indexable public creator profiles, public SFW posts and live room pages; creator growth depends on search and shares
  - Rationale: The current prerenderer only knows a fixed list of 8 pages at build time. Creator profiles, posts and live status are dynamic, numerous and change constantly. The existing seam (entry-server render() plus a pure prerenderFiles() with an injected render) is already the right shape. Generalizing it keeps logic in shared/server-core and makes the edge or Node process just another renderer, which fits principle 4 without a new framework or npm dependency (react-dom/server is already present). Hydrating public routes instead of dropping and remounting avoids double rendering and layout flash on mobile.
  - Risks: Request-time rendering makes public pages depend on the API, which reverses the owner's documented 'no SSR process' stance and is the owner's decision. Mitigations: edge caching with stale-while-revalidate and stale-if-error, and falling back to the plain SPA shell on error. Cache keys must never carry personalized or softcore data.
  - Alternatives: On-publish static generation: a worker job renders HTML to S3/R2 whenever a profile or post changes. This keeps 'API down ≠ site down' but needs heavy invalidation fan-out.; React Router v7 framework mode with loaders: gives SSR and prerender, but replaces the custom router, adds new deps and needs owner approval.; TanStack Start: selective SSR per route, but replaces the router and query layer.
- **Edge head/Open Graph injection for creator, post and live URLs (Cloudflare Worker in front of the existing static assets)** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Phase A (MVP): set run_worker_first to public patterns only (for example /@:handle, /p/:id, /live/:handle). The Worker fetches GET /api/public/cards/:path and injects title, description, canonical, og:image (pre-generated 1200x630 SFW card), twitter:card summary_large_image, robots, and JSON-LD (ProfilePage/Person; VideoObject with BroadcastEvent when live). Cache at the edge and purge on profile or post change. Phase B: render page content with the shared PublicRoute renderer. Generate OG card images with the existing sharp pipeline in workers on profile or go-live events instead of adding Satori.
  - Requirement: Rich link previews on social networks and messaging apps, plus fast indexability for creator growth
  - Rationale: Social and messaging crawlers read only the raw HTML <head>, so today every creator link would preview as the generic 'BSLT' shell. The web app already deploys as Cloudflare Workers static assets. Adding a Worker with run_worker_first limited to public creator routes, which fetches a small public 'page card' endpoint and rewrites <head> with HTMLRewriter, is the cheapest way to get correct previews and canonical/robots tags without touching the SPA. It can later host the full per-request renderer from the seam above. Workers Free allows only 10 ms CPU per request, which is fine for HTMLRewriter but too little for React SSR. Workers Paid defaults to 30 s.
  - Risks: Softcore creators' cards must use an SFW, creator-approved image or a generic card, never softcore media. If the Worker caches a personalized response, it leaks across users. The Cloudflare deploy must not be the only path; keep the Caddy/Node equivalent.
  - Alternatives: Node renderer process behind Caddy for non-Cloudflare deploys, using the same PublicRoute code; Rely on Googlebot's JS rendering alone: covers Google only, no social previews
- **Head/meta builder and sitemaps** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Move the head builder into a pure shared module used by the prerenderer, the edge Worker and a future renderer, and add og:image (absolute, with width and height), og:site_name, twitter:card, robots noindex/nofollow, and an optional rating meta. Serve a dynamic, paginated sitemap index from the API or Worker for public SFW creators and posts. Fail the production build when SITE_URL is missing.
  - Requirement: SEO and rich previews; correct crawler signals for gated content
  - Rationale: The head builder in entry-server.tsx is sound (escaping, absolute-only canonical, escaped JSON-LD) but has no og:image, og:site_name, twitter:card, robots/noindex or rating support. Sitemaps are build-time only and absent when SITE_URL is unset (the current dist has none), which does not scale to thousands of creator pages.
  - Risks: Google says to apply rating=adult only to sexually explicit pages, and SafeSearch filters every page that carries it. Do not apply it blanket-wide to the SFW host.
  - Alternatives: Hand-maintained per-page tags (drifts)
- **Boot splash over server-rendered content** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Skip the splash when #root holds server-rendered content (data-prerendered or SSR), and don't block public routes on authSettled. Show auth-dependent chrome (follow or subscribe buttons) progressively.
  - Requirement: Fast mobile web: LCP on public creator pages
  - Rationale: The opaque splash covers prerendered HTML until the auth session restore and window load finish, so visitors on mobile networks see a spinner even though the content is already in the document. That throws away the main user-facing benefit of server-rendered public pages.
  - Risks: Small layout shift when auth-aware controls appear; reserve space for them.
  - Alternatives: Keep the splash only for authenticated app routes
- **Bundle shape / performance budgets** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, M. Give public routes a lean public-shell entry, either a second Vite input or route-level dynamic imports so the app shell isn't preloaded. Fix the Lighthouse CI job and add budgets for the public creator route, for example ≤100 KB gz JS, LCP <2.5 s and CLS <0.1 on mobile emulation. Keep Vite 8 manualChunks.
  - Requirement: Fast mobile web as the primary mobile surface (app stores restrict this category)
  - Rationale: Every route, public ones included, preloads about 252 KB gzip of JS and CSS, including Table, Select, Modal, pagination and the SaaS shell. That is acceptable for dashboards but heavy for a creator profile opened from a social link on mid-range Android. The Lighthouse CI job has no budgets and is failing.
  - Risks: Two entries means two shells to keep visually consistent; share @bslt/ui tokens.
  - Alternatives: Accept the current shape and rely on the SW cache for repeat visits (does not help first-visit growth traffic)

In the creator fork, never persist entitlements, purchases, DMs, tips, softcore feeds or media URLs, and purge IndexedDB on logout, account switch and leaving softcore mode.
  - Requirement: Privacy basics; SFW users must never see softcore; paywalled content must not linger on shared devices
  - Rationale: The policy is a deny-list: any new query key is written to IndexedDB unless someone remembers to exclude it. On a platform with paid, softcore and messaging data, that default fails open.
  - Risks: Some offline convenience is lost for keys nobody marks.
  - Alternatives: Disable persistence entirely on the creator fork
- **Service worker caching** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Limit SW caching to build assets (/assets/* fingerprinted files, icons, fonts) and honor Cache-Control: private/no-store. Never cache user-content paths. Call clearCache() on logout and user switch. Keep the push handlers.
  - Requirement: Privacy, moderation takedowns, entitlement revocation, and SFW/softcore separation on shared devices
  - Rationale: sw.js caches every same-origin image (/uploads/_.jpg etc.) with stale-while-revalidate, ignoring Cache-Control and auth. Nothing calls clearCache on logout. Paid or moderated media served from the app origin would survive takedown, refund or subscription lapse and remain readable on the device. API responses are safe today only because the server sends no-store on /api/_.

Add an in-app install prompt with display-mode detection and an iOS 'Add to Home Screen' guide, and ask for push permission only after install and a clear trigger such as following a creator. In the creator fork, use generic, SFW lock-screen push text by default (no content previews from softcore creators).
  - Requirement: Mobile web/PWA as the primary mobile surface; creator notifications such as go-live alerts
  - Rationale: The manifest and push plumbing exist, but install UX is missing. On iOS, web push only works for sites added to the Home Screen and there is no automatic install banner, so go-live notifications depend on a guided install.
  - Risks: Install rates on iOS stay low without in-product prompts.

- **CSP and Permissions-Policy** — keep-with-changes, foundation-now, S. Allow camera, microphone and display-capture only on creator broadcast routes. With hls.js, load the transmuxer worker from a same-origin file (workerPath) rather than blob:, so worker-src can stay 'self'. Verify that option against the hls.js version you pick.
  - Requirement: Live player and media CDN, browser go-live, hosted payment and age-verification flows
  - Rationale: Today's CSP allows images, media and XHR only from 'self', so a media CDN, HLS segment hosts, an age-verification vendor or payment iframes would all be blocked. Caddy's Permissions-Policy camera=() and microphone=() blocks browser-based broadcasting (WebRTC/WHIP) from creators' phones. The policy is copied into four places.
  - Risks: Loosening by hand per vendor drifts; keep the drift audit.
  - Alternatives: Per-route CSP at the edge Worker
- **Video / live player** — add-new, creator-mvp, M. Add hls.js plus a <VideoPlayer> in @bslt/ui with custom controls. Use native HLS on Safari/iOS and hls.js elsewhere. Build the live room UI (tip menu, goals, viewer list, chat) as product components on top.
  - Requirement: Live streaming (public rooms, private and group shows, recordings into PPV) on mobile and desktop web
  - Rationale: The only playback is a plain <video src>. Live rooms need HLS/LL-HLS in browsers without native HLS (Chrome, Android, Firefox) and a WebRTC (WHEP) path for low-latency private shows. A thin custom player on @bslt/ui tokens matches the owner's minimal-dependency style. hls.js is mature, Apache-2.0 licensed and the only new npm dependency needed (owner approval required).
  - Risks: LL-HLS gives seconds of latency, which is fine for public rooms but can feel slow for tip-to-reaction in private shows; WebRTC playback depends on the video vendor. Video.js v10 maturity should be rechecked before choosing it.
  - Alternatives: Video.js v10: Mux-led rewrite uniting Video.js, Plyr, Vidstack and Media Chrome. Beta shipped March 2026; GA unconfirmed; v10 drops compatibility with existing plugins.; Vidstack standalone: being folded into Video.js v10, so maintenance risk.; Shaka Player: better for DASH and DRM, heavier.; Vendor player SDK from whichever video provider the video-infra review picks
- **Image delivery (responsive variants, placeholders, blurred previews for locked content)** — augment, creator-mvp, M. Serve originals and full-quality variants of locked media only through short-lived signed URLs issued after an entitlement check. Extend <Image> with placeholder, fetchpriority, width/height and <picture>. Check whether a display:none + loading=lazy image ever starts loading across browsers when a fallback is set.
  - Requirement: Fast mobile feeds and profiles; blurred teasers for PPV, subscription and softcore-locked content

Don't allow on-the-fly flexible variants for locked media.; imgproxy self-hosted (OSS; blur and signed URLs in the free edition); Phase 2: on-the-fly CDN transformations for scale

Enforcement stays in server-core.
  - Requirement: Age-gate interstitial for softcore; reusable for regional blocks or consent in future platforms
  - Rationale: The ToS gate is already a server-signalled requirement that drives a client interstitial. Generalizing it into typed 'access requirements' (tos, age_assurance, region, consent) gives the foundation a generic, server-enforced pattern instead of an ad hoc creator-only overlay.

  - Requirement: SFW by default; SFW users must never see softcore; age verification for the softcore section; SFW share previews
  - Rationale: Nothing in web, client or shared models content rating or audience mode. Serving softcore from a separate hostname, using the same build with a runtime audience flag, isolates cookies, IndexedDB, SW caches, CSP, analytics and search classification. SFW links then never preview softcore, and the SFW domain's search standing is protected.   - Risks: Cross-host auth adds complexity. Age-assurance vendor flows (chosen by another lens) must fit the CSP and redirect model.

- **Paywall / checkout UI** — keep-with-changes, creator-mvp, M. Keep Stripe.js lazy and only on routes that still use it. Add processor and crypto-gateway hosts to the config-driven CSP (form-action and frame-src only if iframes are used).
  - Requirement: Paywalls and redirects to hosted payment pages (high-risk processors, crypto fallback)
  - Rationale: The billing/success and billing/cancel return routes are the right pattern. Creator purchase flows must not be built on Stripe Elements: Stripe's list prohibits adult content and services, including pay-per-view and AI-generated adult content, and treats creator platforms as restricted, needing extra due diligence and possibly refused.

  - Alternatives: Embedded card forms per processor (more PCI and CSP surface)

  - Requirement: Live tipping, goals, tip menus, viewer lists and chat in public rooms and private shows
  - Rationale: The current socket is invalidation-only: it sends no payload and every client refetches over HTTP. For a room with thousands of viewers, each tip or chat message would set off thousands of GETs (a thundering herd), and chat ordering and tip animations need event payloads.
  - Risks: Unbounded room event streams on mobile need throttling and batching in the UI.

### Gaps

- No request-time or edge rendering for dynamic public pages: creator profiles, public posts and live rooms would ship as an empty SPA shell to crawlers and social link previewers.
- Head tags have no og:image, twitter:card or robots/noindex, and there is no dynamic sitemap; the build-time sitemap is missing when SITE_URL is unset.
- QueryCache has no dehydrate/hydrate, so server-rendered public pages can't hand data to the client; the client currently drops prerendered markup and remounts.
- No content-rating or audience (SFW/softcore) model and no age-gate interstitial anywhere in web, client or shared.
- No HLS/LL-HLS/WebRTC player and no live room UI (tip menus, goals, viewer list, chat).
- No placeholder (LQIP) or blurred-teaser derivative pipeline wired to the UI, and no entitlement-gated signed media delivery in the web client.
- The realtime client is invalidation-only, with no payload-bearing room channel for live events.
- No mobile consumer shell or install-prompt UX; the iOS push path depends on Home Screen install.
- No enforced mobile performance budgets; the Lighthouse CI job has no config and is failing.
- CSP and Permissions-Policy are hard-coded and block external media CDNs, HLS segment hosts, age-verification or payment iframes, and browser camera/mic for going live.
- The service worker caches same-origin user images without regard to Cache-Control or auth, and never purges on logout; query persistence fails open (deny-list).

# Appendix B — contrarian challenger

Keep the stack: TypeScript, Fastify 5, Postgres with custom SQL and RLS, React 19 SPA, Turborepo and pnpm. None of the five alternatives tackles what actually makes this product hard: live video, high-risk and crypto payments, verified age gating, moderation, and vendors who accept adult-adjacent content. Those are integration and vendor problems, and the backend language barely matters to them. Every switch throws away a large tested codebase the owner knows. Two of them (Supabase, and Next.js hosted on Vercel) add deplatforming exposure through acceptable-use clauses. Phoenix's real realtime advantage shows only well beyond MVP scale, and Go is best used as off-the-shelf infrastructure (LiveKit, Centrifugo), not as code you write. Next.js's one real benefit, dynamic public SSR, can be added through BSLT's existing SSR seam. The stack is still not ready as it stands. The genuine gaps to close are a channels, presence and payload-broadcast layer with a Redis broker (foundation-now), with the live-room protocol on top (creator-mvp); a live-video provider port (creator-mvp); a general payments port and double-entry ledger (foundation-now), with high-risk and crypto adapters, tips, PPV, entitlements and payouts (creator-mvp), because Stripe and PayPal prohibit the softcore side; runtime SSR and OG for public SFW pages (foundation-now seam); S3 multipart uploads and DB type codegen (foundation-now); RLS-enforced content-mode isolation (creator-mvp); and moving the Rust experiment out of the foundation. These are recommendations only. Each new npm package or architecture change needs the owner's approval.

## (1) Next.js App Router, full-stack TypeScript (RSC, server actions, middleware), keeping Postgres

- Strongest case: Discovery and referrals depend on public creator and post pages that search engines can index and that show link previews on X, Reddit, Discord and Telegram. BSLT only prerenders at build time, and its own module says it covers only pages "identical for every visitor" (main/apps/web/src/ssr/prerender.ts). A creator page like /@name is therefore an empty SPA shell to crawlers and link unfurlers. Next.js gives per-request SSR and streaming, per-creator OG images, image optimisation and edge middleware for geo-specific age-gate rules. It is still React and TypeScript, so @bslt/ui and @bslt/api could carry over.
- Beats current: Runtime SSR and OG metadata for dynamic public SFW pages (creator profiles, post teasers, live-room landing pages). BSLT has no runtime SSR process at all.; Built-in image optimisation and route-level code splitting for media-heavy discovery feeds.; Middleware gives a single place for geo and jurisdiction routing ahead of the age gate.; Larger hiring pool and more examples than BSLT's custom router (main/client/react/src/router) and custom query layer (main/client/react/src/query).
- Loses: Most of the product is authenticated and interactive: live rooms, the player, chat, tipping, the creator studio, the wallet. That is SPA territory, so SSR helps only a thin public slice, and paywalled or softcore content cannot be publicly cached or prerendered anyway.; It works against owner principle 4 (frameworks are renderers): server actions and RSC pull business logic into the rendering layer. The existing Fastify API and typed contracts would still be needed for the realtime, webhook and payment paths.; Security surface: CVE-2025-55182/66478 ('React2Shell', CVSS 10, unauthenticated RCE through RSC payload decoding, Dec 2025) and CVE-2025-29927 (CVSS 9.1 middleware authorisation bypass, Mar 2025). For a business whose age gate and paywall are legal obligations, a middleware-based gate is exactly the wrong place for enforcement. Entitlement has to be enforced in the API and in signed media URLs regardless.; Hosting: Vercel's AUP lets it ban use for an 'objectionable purpose' on non-Enterprise plans, a real deplatforming risk for an adult-adjacent platform. Self-hosting avoids that but removes much of the convenience.; It would replace the custom router and query layer that the web app (about 98k lines including tests) is built on, and with them a tested auth flow (AuthService, about 900 LOC).
- Switching cost: Moderate-to-high. Fastify, core and db could stay, but main/apps/web (about 98k lines including tests) and main/client/react's router and query layer would be reworked into Next routing and data patterns. That is roughly 1–3 months of solo work, plus a fresh security review of the SSR, server-action and middleware surface. Next is a new major dependency and needs owner approval.
- Verdict: Do not switch. Take the one real benefit, dynamic public SSR, by extending BSLT's existing SSR seam (main/apps/web/src/entry-server.tsx, ssr/ServerApp.tsx, SsrContext). Add a small runtime renderer or OG-meta injector for an allowlist of public routes. FOUNDATION-NOW: a generic 'public dynamic route' SSR and meta seam. CREATOR-MVP: SFW-only creator and post share pages. Softcore pages get noindex and no OG image, so SFW users and link unfurlers never see softcore previews.

## (2) Keep TypeScript, but swap Fastify plus custom SQL for NestJS with Drizzle (or Prisma)

- Strongest case: The creator fork adds roughly 40–60 tables: creators, tiers, fan subscriptions, entitlements, posts, media assets, PPV purchases, tips, wallet and ledger entries, payouts, streams, shows and tickets, paid messages, reports, moderation queue, age and KYC records, referrals and analytics rollups. In BSLT each table needs five hand-written pieces: a SQL migration, Record, New and Update interfaces, a COLUMNS map, a repository with toCamelCase and toSnakeCase, and exact-SQL tests (see main/server/db/src/schema/tasks.ts and repositories/tasks/tasks.ts). Builder joins take raw SqlFragment strings and return Record<string, unknown> (builder/select.ts innerJoin). Drift is a real enough risk that the repo needs schema-coverage.test.ts. Drizzle would provide schema-as-code with inferred types, typed joins, generated migrations and Postgres RLS policy declarations. NestJS would add a standard module, DI, guard and interceptor structure that new hires already know.
- Beats current: Far less per-table boilerplate and no TS-vs-SQL drift, because types are inferred from the schema.; Typed joins and selects, where the current builder returns untyped rows from joins.; Generated migrations and an ecosystem of tooling (studio, seeding, codegen).; NestJS conventions are familiar to contractors, which helps if the team grows.
- Loses: Money code wants explicit SQL: SELECT … FOR UPDATE, idempotency keys (already in the queue, migration 0915), double-entry constraints and SKIP LOCKED workers (queue/postgres-store.ts). The custom SQL layer is a strength for the ledger, tips and payouts, not a weakness.; RLS through per-transaction SET LOCAL app.user_id, tenant_id and role (main/server/db/src/client.ts) is the right mechanism to guarantee at the database level that SFW users never see softcore. Prisma handles per-transaction session variables awkwardly, and Drizzle can do it but would not improve on it.; NestJS's decorators and DI duplicate what route-modules plus server/core already provide and contradict the frameworks-are-renderers principle. It brings no realtime, video or payments capability.; None of the product's hard problems (live video, high-risk payments, age verification, moderation) gets easier.
- Switching cost: High for little product gain. The repositories (about 20 domains) would be rewritten, the repository test suites with exact-SQL assertions invalidated, and route and handler wiring re-plumbed in apps/server (about 14k lines). Estimate 1–3 months, and it needs owner approval for new core dependencies and an architecture change.
- Verdict: Reject NestJS outright. Do not migrate to Drizzle or Prisma either. Attack the real pain, per-table boilerplate, with an in-house generator that introspects the migrated schema (information_schema) and emits the Record, New, Update and COLUMNS types, checked by CI as a drift gate. That needs no new npm package and fits the 'static surfaces are checked in CI' principle. FOUNDATION-NOW.

## (3) Elixir/Phoenix (Channels, Presence, PubSub, LiveView, Ecto, Oban) for a realtime-heavy creator platform

- Strongest case: A live-first platform is mostly rooms full of concurrently connected viewers exchanging small, ordered events (chat, tips, goal progress, tip-menu triggers, viewer lists, show start and stop). That is the BEAM's home turf. Each room can be a supervised process holding goal and tip-menu state. Presence gives replicated viewer lists without a central store. PubSub fans out across nodes without Redis. The Phoenix team held 2M websocket connections in one chat room on a single 40-core, 128 GB box (2015), and Supabase's Realtime service is itself built on Phoenix. LiveView could cover the creator studio and live dashboards, Oban covers Postgres-backed jobs, and Membrane offers media pipelines.
- Beats current: BSLT's realtime is an invalidation bus. Frames are {type:'update', key, version} with no payload (main/apps/web/src/lib/realtime.ts). There is no presence and no room broadcast, and the cross-instance broker is Postgres NOTIFY (main/server/db/src/pubsub/postgres-pubsub.ts). Phoenix provides payload broadcast, presence and a cross-node broker out of the box.; Fault isolation per room and backpressure under bursty tip and chat traffic.; Viewer lists, typing indicators and live counters come with no extra infrastructure.
- Loses: It means rewriting the whole backend: server/* (about 93k lines including tests) plus the server half of main/shared (about 81k lines of cross-runtime contracts). The single-language, end-to-end typed contract model is lost. It would have to be rebuilt as cross-language codegen, which breaks principle 1 in its current form.; The React web client stays regardless (player, rich UI, @bslt/ui at about 28k lines), so the result is two languages and two toolchains for a solo owner who knows TypeScript.; Vendor SDK reach is thinner. LiveKit lists server SDKs for Node, Go, Ruby, Java, Python, Rust and PHP but not Elixir. Payment and verification vendors tend to be REST plus a Node SDK.; Its advantage only matters well beyond MVP concurrency. At hundreds to low thousands of concurrent viewers per room, Node plus a Redis broker is adequate.; Tips are money, so they must commit to the Postgres ledger before broadcast. The realtime tier sits downstream of the core whatever language it uses.
- Switching cost: Very high. A full rewrite is roughly 6–12+ months solo, and about 250k lines of tested TypeScript the owner knows would be discarded. A hybrid with Phoenix only as the realtime gateway costs about a month plus permanent second-language ops, auth-token sharing and duplicated permission logic (realtime/permissions is about 900 LOC).
- Verdict: Not for this owner or this MVP. Its real advantage (rooms, presence, fan-out) can be matched in TypeScript: FOUNDATION-NOW, generic channels plus presence plus payload broadcast with a pluggable broker (Postgres for dev, Redis for prod; ioredis is already a dependency of @bslt/db and Redis is in the prod compose). CREATOR-MVP, the live-room protocol. If fan-out ever outgrows Node, adopt an off-the-shelf realtime server (option 4) before considering a language change. Phoenix is LATER at most.

## (4) Go services for the realtime and live tier, with the TypeScript app for everything else

- Strongest case: The live-video ecosystem is largely written in Go: LiveKit's SFU (Apache-2.0, built on Pion, distributed and multi-region, with Ingress and Egress for OBS ingest and recording), Centrifugo (Apache-2.0, channels, presence, history and recovery, Redis or NATS broker) and MediaMTX. Go handles many connections per process, ships as a single static binary and is efficient at fan-out. Splitting the realtime and live tier out isolates bursty traffic from the API and payments path.
- Beats current: BSLT has no live video at all. Media is VOD-only: an ffmpeg wrapper with createHLSStream from a file, plus sharp (main/server/media). There is no RTMP or WHIP ingest, no SFU for private and group shows, no LL-HLS and no live-to-recording pipeline.; The memory and CPU cost per connection and the fan-out throughput of a Go gateway beat Node's single event loop at scale.; Separates the failure domain of live rooms from billing and API.
- Loses: The hard part of the tier is the media plane, and nobody should write that. You run LiveKit, IVS or Cloudflare Stream as a product. The app's language is irrelevant to it: LiveKit has a Node server SDK, and IVS and Stream are REST.; The interaction plane must call the TypeScript core for anything involving money (tip, then ledger, then broadcast), so a hand-written Go service becomes a thin relay. Centrifugo already is that relay, with no Go code to write.; A second language adds toolchain, CI and duplicated auth and permission rules. The repo's Rust experiment (main/apps/server-rust, about 1.85k LOC) shows the cost: after its foundation PR it is still a loopback-only dev-auth slice with its own migrations, excluded from the Core edition (docs/architecture/core-and-extensions.md:119), and it does nothing for the live tier.
- Switching cost: Low if Go means adopting Go-built infrastructure (a LiveKit self-host or a Centrifugo binary driven by JWTs minted by Fastify): days to weeks. Moderate and ongoing if it means writing and maintaining a custom Go service: weeks to build, plus a permanent second-language tax.
- Verdict: Adopt the Go ecosystem as black-box infrastructure. Do not write Go. CREATOR-MVP: a LiveVideoProvider port in server/core with one adapter (LiveKit self-hosted or Amazon IVS; LiveKit's AUP bans only unlawful or non-consensual sexual content but keeps 'obscene/objectionable' and 'harmful to minors' clauses, so get written confirmation for the softcore use case). PHASE-2: Centrifugo as the broker and gateway if the TypeScript channel layer hits its limits. FOUNDATION-NOW: move the Rust app out of the foundation (its own repo or branch); it is unrelated to this product and confuses the 'one language' story.

## (5) BaaS approach: Supabase (Auth, Postgres plus RLS, Storage, Realtime, Edge Functions)

- Strongest case: Fastest path to an MVP. Managed Postgres with RLS, auth, storage with signed URLs and image transforms, and Realtime Broadcast and Presence (itself built on Phoenix) are all available on day one. Supabase's AUP (v1, June 2026) explicitly bans only CSAM and non-consensual or deceptive sexual imagery, not legal adult content. The model is also Postgres plus RLS, the same mental model BSLT uses.
- Beats current: Presence and payload broadcast out of the box, the two realtime primitives BSLT lacks.; Managed backups, PITR, pooling and a storage CDN, which means less ops than Docker Compose plus Terraform.; Image transformations and signed URLs without running sharp workers.
- Loses: Realtime quotas: Pro defaults to 500 concurrent connections and 500 msg/s, rising to 10,000 and 2,500 only with the spend cap removed. Usage is billed per message and per peak connection. One popular tipping room can blow through that, and tip and chat traffic becomes a metered cost.; Clause 6.1 reserves discretion over 'reputational' risk. For an adult-adjacent business, building auth, data and money on one vendor's discretion is the worst deplatforming exposure available.; Its client-queries-plus-RLS-plus-edge-functions model inverts BSLT principle 4, which keeps logic in server/core. Entitlements, ledger and payouts belong in a server you control.; BSLT's auth is already stronger for high-value creator accounts that are targets for payout hijacking: passkeys, TOTP, sudo mode, device and session revocation, backup codes.; It does not touch the real blockers: high-risk payments, age verification, moderation, live video.
- Switching cost: High. It discards or rewrites core/auth, the db layer, realtime and storage (well over 100k lines including tests) and rewires web data access. Using Supabase only as a managed Postgres host is cheap and compatible, but offers little over the DigitalOcean and GCP managed Postgres already covered by Terraform.
- Verdict: Reject as the platform. At most it could be a managed Postgres host (LATER, optional). Build the missing presence and broadcast primitives in BSLT instead (FOUNDATION-NOW).

### Real weaknesses of the current stack

- Repo root for all paths below: \\wsl.localhost\ubuntu-24.04\home\abe\projects\library\bslt\bslt-main. REALTIME IS AN INVALIDATION BUS, NOT A LIVE-ROOM SYSTEM. The server sends only {type:'update', key, version} (main/apps/web/src/lib/realtime.ts; PubSubMessage in main/server/db/src/pubsub/postgres-pubsub.ts), and clients refetch over HTTP. There is no presence, no viewer list and no room broadcast with payloads, and the client sync engine was removed on 2026-06-12 (docs/DIRECTION.md). Failure mode: one tip in a 2,000-viewer room means 2,000 HTTP refetches. The cross-instance broker is Postgres NOTIFY, which takes a global lock at commit (DBOS measured a latency knee around 2.5k msg/s) and caps payloads at 8 KB. Fix: FOUNDATION-NOW, channels plus presence plus payload broadcast with a pluggable broker (keep Postgres for dev, add a Redis adapter; ioredis is already a dependency). CREATOR-MVP: the live-room protocol (chat, tips, goals, tip menus, viewer lists, rate limits, ordered tip events after ledger commit). PHASE-2: Centrifugo if needed.
- NO LIVE VIDEO. main/server/media is VOD-only: ffmpeg transcode, createHLSStream from a file, sharp images. There is no ingest (RTMP or WHIP), no SFU for private and group shows, no LL-HLS and no live-to-PPV recording. This has to be bought, not built. CREATOR-MVP: a LiveVideoProvider port with one adapter (LiveKit self-hosted or Amazon IVS; IVS Real-Time bills per participant-hour, Low-Latency per input-hour plus viewer-hour output). Get the provider's acceptance of softcore use in writing. Mux's AUP could not be verified (page 404).
- BILLING PORT IS SHAPED FOR SAAS SUBSCRIPTIONS, AND BOTH ADAPTERS' PROCESSORS PROHIBIT THE ADULT SIDE. BillingService (main/shared/src/modules/core/billing/billing.service.types.ts) models a platform selling plans: checkout, subscriptions, invoices, products and prices. Its NormalizedEventType has only subscription, invoice, refund and chargeback events. There are no one-time PPV purchases, tips or wallet, no per-creator tiers or entitlements, no ledger and no payouts. Stripe lists adult content and adult live-chat as prohibited and content-creation platforms as restricted; PayPal bars sexually oriented digital goods. Fix: FOUNDATION-NOW, generalise to a payments port (one-time and stored-token charges, refunds, chargebacks, purchase events) plus a generic double-entry ledger module. CREATOR-MVP: high-risk processor adapter(s) (CCBill exposes a REST API with OAuth, payment tokens and webhooks; Segpay and Epoch are alternatives), crypto rails, a wallet or credits model so each tip is not a card transaction, creator entitlements, payout batches.
- NO DYNAMIC PUBLIC RENDERING. The web app is a Vite SPA, and the prerender is build-time only, for prose 'identical for every visitor' (main/apps/web/src/ssr/prerender.ts). Creator and post pages would reach crawlers and link unfurlers as empty shells, which hurts SFW discovery and referrals. FOUNDATION-NOW: a runtime SSR or meta-injection seam for allowlisted public routes, reusing entry-server.tsx. CREATOR-MVP: SFW-only share pages, with softcore set to noindex and given no previews.
- LARGE MEDIA UPLOADS. main/server/storage offers presigned PUT and GET and local and S3 providers, but no S3 multipart or resumable upload. Multi-GB recordings and uploads need it. FOUNDATION-NOW.
- DB BOILERPLATE AND UNTYPED JOINS. Every table needs a hand-written migration, Record, New and Update interfaces, a COLUMNS map and a repository (main/server/db/src/schema/tasks.ts). Joins take raw SqlFragment strings and return Record<string, unknown>. With about 40–60 new tables this is the biggest velocity tax. FOUNDATION-NOW: an in-house schema-introspection codegen plus a drift gate (no ORM).
- AGE ASSURANCE IS ATTESTATION ONLY. Migration 0916 records a self-attested 13+/16+ statement and deliberately stores no date of birth and does no verification. The stack does not block verified 18+ gating, but none exists yet. CREATOR-MVP: an AgeVerificationProvider port plus an RLS-enforced content-mode flag (see strengths).
- ORPHAN SECOND BACKEND. main/apps/server-rust (about 1.85k LOC, its own migrations, loopback-only dev auth for an unrelated product, excluded from the Core edition) adds confusion and a second toolchain to a foundation meant to be generic TypeScript. FOUNDATION-NOW: move it out. This is the owner's call.
- Scale ceiling is real but distant. A single Node event loop per process, a Postgres job queue and Postgres pub/sub are fine for the MVP. Heavy ffmpeg and moderation work must run in separate worker processes or containers, not the API process. PHASE-2: separate media and moderation worker deployment, and a Redis or NATS broker.

### Real strengths

- Postgres with hand-written SQL and RLS through per-transaction SET LOCAL app.user_id, tenant_id and role (main/server/db/src/client.ts, migration 0900_rls.sql). This is the strongest available mechanism for the product's hardest invariant, 'SFW users never see softcore'. Add an app.content_mode / app.age_verified session variable and enforce visibility in the database, not just in the UI. FOUNDATION-NOW: generalise the session-variable allowlist. CREATOR-MVP: content-rating RLS policies.
- Explicit SQL suits money. FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED queue (db/src/queue/postgres-store.ts), idempotency keys (migration 0915), transactions via withTransaction, leader-elected cron with advisory locks (workers/src/scheduler). This is the right toolkit for a double-entry ledger, tip settlement, chargeback reversals and payout batches, and an ORM would add little.
- End-to-end typed contracts. Shared schema objects in main/shared, a generated client and hooks (main/client/api, main/client/react) and API-governance drift audits. These pay off in a product with many endpoints touching money and entitlements.
- Auth suited to high-value creator accounts that are targets for payout hijacking: passkeys, TOTP, SMS, sudo mode for sensitive operations, device and session revocation, backup codes, API keys, OAuth (main/server/core/src/auth).
- Fits web-first distribution: Web Push (VAPID) end to end with a service worker. This is the main re-engagement channel when app stores are closed to this category. Also in-app notifications, signed outbound webhooks and i18n with 9 locales.
- Compliance plumbing already exists: account deletion with PII anonymisation and hard-ban anonymisation workers, audit log, ToS gating, eligibility-attestation evidence, consent console. These are good bases for privacy, bans and moderation evidence trails.
- Media pipeline for the VOD half: ffmpeg transcode, HLS packaging, thumbnails and sharp image processing (including blurred locked-content previews), behind a job queue. Recordings-to-PPV can reuse it once a live provider delivers the recording files.
- Single language, owner-written and heavily tested: about 252k non-test and about 248k test lines of TypeScript (measured), an adversarial TDD culture, Vitest and Playwright, real-DB integration gates. That is a large head start no alternative can match. Also already hosted on providers that do not ban legal adult content outright (self-managed compose with Caddy on DO or GCP, Cloudflare for web). Confirm in writing anyway.
- Capability profiles and recipes let the foundation stay generic while the creator fork turns on realtime, media, workers and billing. This matches the planned restructure-then-fork.
